Phoenix ⇄ Minneapolis

PHX to MSP turbulence

Flights between Phoenix (PHX) and Minneapolis (MSP) fly about 1,280 miles, roughly 2¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, and more often in May, June and October.

A year of pilot reports

Strong bumps are rare

Only 58 of 18,432 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.3%). Light chop was the level reported most: 8,717 reports.

Takeoff and landing

PHX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 68 days in the past year, most often in June (13 days). MSP had gusts of 25 knots or more on 127 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). Questions

Is PHX to MSP a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the PHX–MSP route filed 18,432 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 58 of 18,432 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between PHX and MSP?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, near Colorado Springs, about 1 h after leaving PHX, or 1 h 40 min after leaving MSP. The next most were over Nebraska, about 1 h 55 min after leaving PHX, or 45 min after leaving MSP.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly PHX to MSP?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in April and July and a bigger share in May, June and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is MSP to PHX the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from PHX to MSP usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at PHX and MSP?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. PHX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 68 days in the past year, most often in June (13 days). MSP had gusts of 25 knots or more on 127 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 18,432 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct PHX–MSP path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
