YYZ to LHR turbulence

Flights between Toronto (YYZ) and London (LHR) fly about 3,550 miles, roughly 7 hours in the air. Pilots file few turbulence reports on this route, so this page is a model estimate, by season. In a year of weather-model analyses, the air at cruising height was calmer in January and February.

Questions

Is YYZ to LHR a bumpy flight?

Most flights on any airline route see light chop at most, and strong bumps are rare. On the US routes where we can count reports, 0.4% of airliner reports at cruising height were severe. We can't count reports on YYZ–LHR the same way: few are filed outside North America in the archive we use. In the model, the air at cruising height was calmer than usual in January and February. On the day, the weather decides.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly YYZ to LHR?

In a year of weather-model analyses (November 2025 to October 2026), the air at cruising height was calmer in January and February. Treat it as a rough guide: on US routes, the model's calmer and bumpier months matched pilot reports on 7 of the 16 routes we checked. Any month can bring a smooth or a bumpy day.

Is LHR to YYZ the same?

Yes. It's the same air, just at different times into the flight. Flights from YYZ to LHR usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Why doesn't this page say where on the route it's bumpiest?

Because we couldn't check it. On US routes, where pilots file thousands of reports, the stretches the model rated bumpiest over a year didn't match the stretches where pilots reported the most bumps, so we only show months here.

Can I get a turbulence forecast for my YYZ to LHR flight?

Yes, as an approximate outlook. In BumpCast, turn on Global outlooks (beta) in Settings for a turbulence outlook on international flights like YYZ–LHR, from the same two weather models. Within the continental US and southern Canada, the app's forecasts are more detailed. It's free to download on the App Store for iPhone.

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. 1,460 analyses, four a day, from two global weather models: ECMWF's open data (the European model) and NOAA's GFS (the US model), the same two behind the app's Global outlooks.
  • Turbulence: BumpCast's own clear-air turbulence estimate from each model's winds and temperatures (wind shear, stability and waves over mountains), averaged over the two models, read every 5 minutes along a typical YYZ–LHR flight (the direct path, a typical climb, cruise and descent), at the height it would fly.
  • Light bumps or more: an estimate of 0.10 or more on the turbulence scale pilots and forecasters use (eddy dissipation rate), where the app starts calling it light chop. Each month is compared with the route's own average, so these pages don't rank routes against each other.
  • Checked against pilots: we ran the same method over the US and Canada and compared it with 441,678 airliner pilot reports. Report by report, it has real skill (how accurate it is). But over a year, its bumpier and calmer months matched pilot reports on 7 of 16 US routes, and its bumpiest stretches on 2. So these pages show months only, as a rough guide.
  • What it can't see: weather models miss most bumps in and near thunderstorms and over small hills, and pilots steer around storms on the day. Contains ECMWF open data (CC BY 4.0). GFS data from NOAA.
