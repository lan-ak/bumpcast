# YYZ to ORD turbulence

> Toronto (YYZ) ⇄ Chicago (ORD), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Toronto (YYZ) and Chicago (ORD) fly about 430 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Michigan, and more often in March, April and May.

- Distance: 430 miles (700 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 10 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 6,291
- Severe reports: 30 (0.5%)
- Gusty days: YYZ 120, ORD 123

## Questions

### Is YYZ to ORD a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YYZ–ORD route filed 6,291 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 30 of 6,291 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between YYZ and ORD?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Michigan, near Detroit, about 30 min after leaving YYZ, or 35 min after leaving ORD.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly YYZ to ORD?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in December and February and a bigger share in March, April and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is ORD to YYZ the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from ORD to YYZ usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YYZ and ORD?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. YYZ had gusts of 25 knots or more on 120 days in the past year, most often in January (18 days). ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
