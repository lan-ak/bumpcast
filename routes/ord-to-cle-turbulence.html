Chicago ⇄ Cleveland

ORD to CLE turbulence

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and Cleveland (CLE) fly about 320 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, and more often in March and April.

A year of pilot reports

Strong bumps are rare

Only 39 of 6,343 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.6%). Light chop was the level reported most: 2,836 reports.

Takeoff and landing

ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). CLE had gusts of 25 knots or more on 128 days in the past year, most often in March (17 days). Questions

Is ORD to CLE a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–CLE route filed 6,343 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 39 of 6,343 (0.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between ORD and CLE?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving ORD, or 50 min after leaving CLE. The next most were over Indiana, near Grand Rapids, about 15 min after leaving ORD, or 35 min after leaving CLE.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to CLE?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in March and April. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is CLE to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and CLE?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). CLE had gusts of 25 knots or more on 128 days in the past year, most often in March (17 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 6,343 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct ORD–CLE path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
