YVR to YYZ turbulence

Flights between Vancouver (YVR) and Toronto (YYZ) fly about 2,080 miles, roughly 4¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Michigan, and more often in June and August. Severe reports were rare: 21 of 3,769.

Questions

Is YVR to YYZ a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YVR–YYZ route filed 3,769 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 21 of 3,769 (0.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between YVR and YYZ?

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Michigan, about 3 h 40 min after leaving YVR (35 min after leaving YYZ): 164 of 600 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly YVR to YYZ?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in January and February and a bigger share in June and August. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is YYZ to YVR the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from YVR to YYZ usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YVR and YYZ?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. YVR had gusts of 25 knots or more on 32 days in the past year, most often in December (8 days). YYZ had gusts of 25 knots or more on 120 days in the past year, most often in January (18 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 3,769 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct YVR–YYZ path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
