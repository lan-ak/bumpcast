# YYZ to DEN turbulence

> Toronto (YYZ) ⇄ Denver (DEN), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Toronto (YYZ) and Denver (DEN) fly about 1,310 miles, roughly 2¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, and more often in May and June.

- Distance: 1,310 miles (2,110 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 50 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 24,823
- Severe reports: 86 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: YYZ 120, DEN 155

## Questions

### Is YYZ to DEN a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YYZ–DEN route filed 24,823 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 86 of 24,823 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between YYZ and DEN?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 2 h 40 min after leaving YYZ, or 5 min after leaving DEN.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly YYZ to DEN?

Moderate bumps made up a bigger share of reports in May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is DEN to YYZ the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from DEN to YYZ usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YYZ and DEN?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. YYZ had gusts of 25 knots or more on 120 days in the past year, most often in January (18 days). DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
