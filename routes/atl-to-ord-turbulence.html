ATL to ORD turbulence

Flights between Atlanta (ATL) and Chicago (ORD) fly about 610 miles, roughly 1½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Indiana, and more often in March and May. Severe reports were rare: 42 of 17,699.

Questions

Is ATL to ORD a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ATL–ORD route filed 17,699 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 42 of 17,699 (0.2%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between ATL and ORD?

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Indiana, near Indianapolis, about 1 h after leaving ATL (25 min after leaving ORD): 388 of 1,692 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms. The next most were over Kentucky, near Louisville, about 45 min after leaving ATL (40 min after leaving ORD): 302 of 1,579 reports there. When is the smoothest time of year to fly ATL to ORD?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July and September and a bigger share in March and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is ORD to ATL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ATL and ORD?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ATL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 65 days in the past year, most often in March (10 days). ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 17,699 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct ATL–ORD path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
