Seattle ⇄ Minneapolis

SEA to MSP turbulence

Flights between Seattle (SEA) and Minneapolis (MSP) fly about 1,390 miles, roughly 3 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near SEA, and more often in November.

A year of pilot reports

Strong bumps are rare

Only 18 of 6,228 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.3%). Light chop was the level reported most: 2,961 reports.

Takeoff and landing

SEA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 48 days in the past year, most often in December (18 days). MSP had gusts of 25 knots or more on 127 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). Questions

Is SEA to MSP a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the SEA–MSP route filed 6,228 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 18 of 6,228 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between SEA and MSP?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near SEA, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 10 min after leaving SEA, or 2 h 50 min after leaving MSP.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly SEA to MSP?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in May and a bigger share in November. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is MSP to SEA the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from SEA to MSP usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at SEA and MSP?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. SEA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 48 days in the past year, most often in December (18 days). MSP had gusts of 25 knots or more on 127 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 6,228 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct SEA–MSP path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
