DEN to LAX turbulence

Flights between Denver (DEN) and Los Angeles (LAX) fly about 860 miles, roughly 2 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, and more often in December and June. Severe reports were rare: 115 of 14,934.

Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving DEN (1 h 50 min after leaving LAX): 1,686 of 5,113 reports there. Takeoff and landing

DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days). Questions

Is DEN to LAX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DEN–LAX route filed 14,934 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 115 of 14,934 (0.8%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between DEN and LAX?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land (about 5 min after leaving DEN (1 h 50 min after leaving LAX)).

When is the smoothest time of year to fly DEN to LAX?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in December and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is LAX to DEN the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAX to DEN usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DEN and LAX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 14,934 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct DEN–LAX path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
