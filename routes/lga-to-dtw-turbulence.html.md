# LGA to DTW turbulence

> New York (LGA) ⇄ Detroit (DTW), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between New York (LGA) and Detroit (DTW) fly about 500 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over New York, and more often in April.

- Distance: 500 miles (810 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 15 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 5,420
- Severe reports: 55 (1.0%)
- Gusty days: LGA 146, DTW 106

## Questions

### Is LGA to DTW a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LGA–DTW route filed 5,420 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 55 of 5,420 (1.0%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between LGA and DTW?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over New York, near Buffalo, about 40 min after leaving LGA, or 30 min after leaving DTW. The next most were over Pennsylvania, about 30 min after leaving LGA, or 40 min after leaving DTW.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly LGA to DTW?

Moderate bumps made up a bigger share of reports in April. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is DTW to LGA the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from DTW to LGA usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LGA and DTW?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LGA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 146 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days). DTW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 106 days in the past year, most often in January (14 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
