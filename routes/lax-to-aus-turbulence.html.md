# LAX to AUS turbulence

> Los Angeles (LAX) ⇄ Austin (AUS), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Los Angeles (LAX) and Austin (AUS) fly about 1,240 miles, roughly 2¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Texas, and more often in February and May.

- Distance: 1,240 miles (1,990 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 40 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 5,955
- Severe reports: 30 (0.5%)
- Gusty days: LAX 17, AUS 91

## Questions

### Is LAX to AUS a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAX–AUS route filed 5,955 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 30 of 5,955 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between LAX and AUS?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Texas, about 2 h 15 min after leaving LAX, or 25 min after leaving AUS. The next most were over New Mexico, near El Paso, about 1 h 30 min after leaving LAX, or 1 h 10 min after leaving AUS.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAX to AUS?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in February and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is AUS to LAX the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAX to AUS usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAX and AUS?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days). AUS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 91 days in the past year, most often in March (18 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
