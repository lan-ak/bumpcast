# ATL to LGA turbulence

> Atlanta (ATL) ⇄ New York (LGA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Atlanta (ATL) and New York (LGA) fly about 760 miles, roughly 1¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over North Carolina, and more often in May and June. Severe reports were rare: 70 of 12,454.

- Distance: 760 miles (1,220 km)

## Questions

### Is ATL to LGA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ATL–LGA route filed 12,454 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 70 of 12,454 (0.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between ATL and LGA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over North Carolina, near Charlotte (about 30 min after leaving ATL (1 h 10 min after leaving LGA)), and next most over Virginia, near Greensboro (about 45 min after leaving ATL (1 h after leaving LGA)).

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ATL to LGA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July and September and a bigger share in May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is LGA to ATL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from ATL to LGA usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ATL and LGA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ATL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 65 days in the past year, most often in March (10 days). LGA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 146 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
