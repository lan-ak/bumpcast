# BOS to LAX turbulence

> Boston (BOS) ⇄ Los Angeles (LAX), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Boston (BOS) and Los Angeles (LAX) fly about 2,610 miles, roughly 5¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over New York, and more often in May and June.

- Distance: 2,610 miles (4,190 km)
- In the air: about 5 h 20 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 39,807
- Severe reports: 109 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: BOS 148, LAX 17

## Questions

### Is BOS to LAX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the BOS–LAX route filed 39,807 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 109 of 39,807 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between BOS and LAX?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over New York, near Albany, about 30 min after leaving BOS, or 4 h 50 min after leaving LAX. The next most were near Windsor, about 1 h 15 min after leaving BOS, or 4 h after leaving LAX.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly BOS to LAX?

Moderate bumps made up a bigger share of reports in May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is LAX to BOS the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAX to BOS usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at BOS and LAX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. BOS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 148 days in the past year, most often in December (23 days). LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
