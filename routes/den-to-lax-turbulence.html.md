# DEN to LAX turbulence

> Denver (DEN) ⇄ Los Angeles (LAX), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Denver (DEN) and Los Angeles (LAX) fly about 860 miles, roughly 2 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, and more often in December and June. Severe reports were rare: 115 of 14,934.

- Distance: 860 miles (1,390 km)
- In the air: about 2 h (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 14,934
- Severe reports: 115 (0.8%)
- Gusty days: DEN 155, LAX 17

## Questions

### Is DEN to LAX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DEN–LAX route filed 14,934 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 115 of 14,934 (0.8%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between DEN and LAX?

Moderate bumps were reported most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving DEN (1 h 50 min after leaving LAX): 1,686 of 5,113 reports there. Close to the ground, wind over the mountains stirs the air, and gusty days at DEN add to it.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly DEN to LAX?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in December and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is LAX to DEN the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAX to DEN usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DEN and LAX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
