# YVR to SAN turbulence

> Vancouver (YVR) ⇄ San Diego (SAN), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Vancouver (YVR) and San Diego (SAN) fly about 1,180 miles, roughly 2½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Oregon.

- Distance: 1,180 miles (1,900 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 35 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 3,208
- Severe reports: 24 (0.7%)
- Gusty days: YVR 32, SAN 20

## Questions

### Is YVR to SAN a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YVR–SAN route filed 3,208 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 24 of 3,208 (0.7%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Oregon, near Portland, about 30 min after leaving YVR, or 2 h after leaving SAN. The next most were over California, near Ontario, about 2 h 15 min after leaving YVR, or 15 min after leaving SAN.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly YVR to SAN?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in March and July. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is SAN to YVR the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YVR and SAN?

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
