Vancouver ⇄ Seattle

YVR to SEA turbulence

Flights between Vancouver (YVR) and Seattle (SEA) fly about 130 miles, roughly 30 minutes in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near SEA, and more often in November, December and March.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 3 of 419 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.7%). Light chop was the level reported most: 171 reports.

Questions

Is YVR to SEA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YVR–SEA route filed 419 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 3 of 419 (0.7%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. Where is it bumpiest between YVR and SEA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near SEA, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 30 min after leaving YVR, or 0 min after leaving SEA.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly YVR to SEA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in February, April and October and a bigger share in November, December and March. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is SEA to YVR the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YVR and SEA?

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 419 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct YVR–SEA path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
