# SLC to ATL turbulence

> Salt Lake City (SLC) ⇄ Atlanta (ATL), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Salt Lake City (SLC) and Atlanta (ATL) fly about 1,590 miles, roughly 3½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Arkansas, and more often in May and June.

- Distance: 1,590 miles (2,550 km)
- In the air: about 3 h 25 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 22,326
- Severe reports: 103 (0.5%)
- Gusty days: SLC 105, ATL 65

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Arkansas, near Memphis, about 2 h 30 min after leaving SLC, or 45 min after leaving ATL. There, 732 of 1,791 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

The next most were over Colorado, near Denver, about 45 min after leaving SLC, or 2 h 30 min after leaving ATL. There, 581 of 1,980 reports were moderate or stronger. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height.

## Questions

### Is SLC to ATL a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the SLC–ATL route filed 22,326 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 103 of 22,326 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between SLC and ATL?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Arkansas, near Memphis, about 2 h 30 min after leaving SLC, or 45 min after leaving ATL. The next most were over Colorado, near Denver, about 45 min after leaving SLC, or 2 h 30 min after leaving ATL.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly SLC to ATL?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in January and July and a bigger share in May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is ATL to SLC the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from SLC to ATL usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at SLC and ATL?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. SLC had gusts of 25 knots or more on 105 days in the past year, most often in July (14 days). ATL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 65 days in the past year, most often in March (10 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
