# ATL to SAN turbulence

> Atlanta (ATL) ⇄ San Diego (SAN), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Atlanta (ATL) and San Diego (SAN) fly about 1,890 miles, roughly 4 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Mississippi, and more often in May.

- Distance: 1,890 miles (3,040 km)
- In the air: about 4 h (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 20,264
- Severe reports: 75 (0.4%)
- Gusty days: ATL 65, SAN 20

## Questions

### Is ATL to SAN a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ATL–SAN route filed 20,264 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 75 of 20,264 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between ATL and SAN?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Mississippi, near Memphis, about 45 min after leaving ATL, or 3 h 10 min after leaving SAN. The next most were over Texas, about 1 h 50 min after leaving ATL, or 2 h after leaving SAN.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ATL to SAN?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is SAN to ATL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from SAN to ATL usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ATL and SAN?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ATL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 65 days in the past year, most often in March (10 days). SAN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 20 days in the past year, most often in February (4 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
