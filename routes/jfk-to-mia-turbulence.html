JFK to MIA turbulence

Flights between New York (JFK) and Miami (MIA) fly about 1,090 miles, roughly 2½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over North Carolina, and more often in November, December and February. Severe reports were rare: 87 of 11,798.

Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over North Carolina, near Norfolk, about 40 min after leaving JFK (1 h 40 min after leaving MIA): 492 of 738 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms. More on clear-air turbulence and turbulence near clouds.

The next most were near JFK, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving JFK (2 h 15 min after leaving MIA): 364 of 784 reports there. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at JFK add to it. More on bumps near the ground and gusty takeoffs and landings.

Takeoff and landing

JFK had gusts of 25 knots or more on 157 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days). MIA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 58 days in the past year, most often in April (9 days). On a gusty day, expect a few minutes of bumps and some sway on the climb out and the way in to land. Why gusty days are bumpy.

Questions

Is JFK to MIA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the JFK–MIA route filed 11,798 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 87 of 11,798 (0.7%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between JFK and MIA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over North Carolina, near Norfolk (about 40 min after leaving JFK (1 h 40 min after leaving MIA)), and next most near JFK, where flights climb out or come in to land (about 5 min after leaving JFK (2 h 15 min after leaving MIA)).

When is the smoothest time of year to fly JFK to MIA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in November, December and February. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is MIA to JFK the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at JFK and MIA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. JFK had gusts of 25 knots or more on 157 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days). MIA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 58 days in the past year, most often in April (9 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 11,798 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct JFK–MIA path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
