# LGA to YYZ turbulence

> New York (LGA) ⇄ Toronto (YYZ), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between New York (LGA) and Toronto (YYZ) fly about 360 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over New York, and more often in December and October. Severe reports were rare: 38 of 4,788.

- Distance: 360 miles (570 km)
- In the air: about 1 h (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 4,788
- Severe reports: 38 (0.8%)
- Gusty days: LGA 146, YYZ 120

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over New York, near Rochester, about 30 min after leaving LGA (25 min after leaving YYZ): 354 of 1,267 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is LGA to YYZ a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LGA–YYZ route filed 4,788 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 38 of 4,788 (0.8%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between LGA and YYZ?

Moderate bumps were reported most often over New York, near Rochester, about 30 min after leaving LGA (25 min after leaving YYZ): 354 of 1,267 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly LGA to YYZ?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in February and July and a bigger share in December and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is YYZ to LGA the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LGA and YYZ?

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
