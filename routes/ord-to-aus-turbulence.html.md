# ORD to AUS turbulence

> Chicago (ORD) ⇄ Austin (AUS), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and Austin (AUS) fly about 980 miles, roughly 2¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, and more often in March, April and May.

- Distance: 980 miles (1,570 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 10 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 14,247
- Severe reports: 66 (0.5%)
- Gusty days: ORD 123, AUS 91

## Questions

### Is ORD to AUS a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–AUS route filed 14,247 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 66 of 14,247 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between ORD and AUS?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving ORD, or 2 h 5 min after leaving AUS.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to AUS?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in March, April and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is AUS to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from AUS to ORD usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and AUS?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). AUS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 91 days in the past year, most often in March (18 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
