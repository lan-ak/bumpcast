# ORD to STL turbulence

> Chicago (ORD) ⇄ St Louis (STL), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and St Louis (STL) fly about 260 miles, roughly 50 minutes in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, and more often in March, May and June.

- Distance: 260 miles (410 km)
- In the air: about 50 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 5,125
- Severe reports: 29 (0.6%)
- Gusty days: ORD 123, STL 79

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving ORD, or 45 min after leaving STL. There, 193 of 1,500 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at ORD add to it.

The next most were over Illinois, about 20 min after leaving ORD, or 20 min after leaving STL. There, 132 of 481 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is ORD to STL a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–STL route filed 5,125 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 29 of 5,125 (0.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between ORD and STL?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving ORD, or 45 min after leaving STL. The next most were over Illinois, about 20 min after leaving ORD, or 20 min after leaving STL.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to STL?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, September and October and a bigger share in March, May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is STL to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and STL?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). STL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 79 days in the past year, most often in March (17 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
