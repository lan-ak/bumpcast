# CLT to PHL turbulence

> Charlotte (CLT) ⇄ Philadelphia (PHL), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Charlotte (CLT) and Philadelphia (PHL) fly about 450 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Virginia, and more often in June and September.

- Distance: 450 miles (720 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 10 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 3,625
- Severe reports: 38 (1.0%)
- Gusty days: CLT 49, PHL 118

## Questions

### Is CLT to PHL a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the CLT–PHL route filed 3,625 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 38 of 3,625 (1.0%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between CLT and PHL?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Virginia, near Richmond, about 30 min after leaving CLT, or 35 min after leaving PHL. The next most were over North Carolina, near Greensboro, about 15 min after leaving CLT, or 50 min after leaving PHL.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly CLT to PHL?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in January and a bigger share in June and September. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is PHL to CLT the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at CLT and PHL?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. CLT had gusts of 25 knots or more on 49 days in the past year, most often in February (8 days). PHL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 118 days in the past year, most often in May (17 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
