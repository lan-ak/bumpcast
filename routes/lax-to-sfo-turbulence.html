LAX to SFO turbulence

Flights between Los Angeles (LAX) and San Francisco (SFO) fly about 340 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near LAX, and more often in December and March. Severe reports were rare: 13 of 823.

Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often near LAX, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving LAX (55 min after leaving SFO): 73 of 158 reports there. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air. More on bumps near the ground.

Takeoff and landing

LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days). SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days). On a gusty day, expect a few minutes of bumps and some sway on the climb out and the way in to land. Why gusty days are bumpy.

What this means for your flight

A year of reports shows where and when bumps are more likely, not what your flight will be like. That depends on the weather on the day, which is what BumpCast forecasts for your exact flight.

Questions

Is LAX to SFO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAX–SFO route filed 823 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 13 of 823 (1.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between LAX and SFO?

Moderate bumps were reported most often near LAX, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving LAX (55 min after leaving SFO): 73 of 158 reports there. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAX to SFO?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in February, April and August and a bigger share in December and March. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is SFO to LAX the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAX and SFO?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days). SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 823 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct LAX–SFO path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
