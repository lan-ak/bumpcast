BOS to LGA turbulence

Flights between Boston (BOS) and New York (LGA) fly about 180 miles, roughly 40 minutes in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Connecticut, and more often in November and October. Severe reports were rare: 22 of 2,695.

Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Connecticut, near Hartford, about 15 min after leaving BOS (20 min after leaving LGA): 145 of 440 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms. More on clear-air turbulence and turbulence near clouds.

Questions

Is BOS to LGA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the BOS–LGA route filed 2,695 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 22 of 2,695 (0.8%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between BOS and LGA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Connecticut, near Hartford (about 15 min after leaving BOS (20 min after leaving LGA)).

When is the smoothest time of year to fly BOS to LGA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in August and September and a bigger share in November and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is LGA to BOS the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at BOS and LGA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. BOS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 148 days in the past year, most often in December (23 days). LGA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 146 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 2,695 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct BOS–LGA path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
