Atlanta ⇄ Nashville

ATL to BNA turbulence

Flights between Atlanta (ATL) and Nashville (BNA) fly about 210 miles, roughly 45 minutes in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Georgia, and more often in May, June and October.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 5 of 1,979 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.3%). Smooth air was the level reported most: 819 reports.

Questions

Is ATL to BNA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ATL–BNA route filed 1,979 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 5 of 1,979 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between ATL and BNA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Georgia, near Atlanta, about 10 min after leaving ATL, or 25 min after leaving BNA. The next most were over Tennessee, near Nashville, about 25 min after leaving ATL, or 15 min after leaving BNA.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly ATL to BNA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in December, August and September and a bigger share in May, June and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is BNA to ATL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ATL and BNA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ATL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 65 days in the past year, most often in March (10 days). BNA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 97 days in the past year, most often in March (17 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 1,979 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct ATL–BNA path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
