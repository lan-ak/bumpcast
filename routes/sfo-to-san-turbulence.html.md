# SFO to SAN turbulence

> San Francisco (SFO) ⇄ San Diego (SAN), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between San Francisco (SFO) and San Diego (SAN) fly about 450 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over California, and more often in December and March.

- Distance: 450 miles (720 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 10 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 995
- Severe reports: 11 (1.1%)
- Gusty days: SFO 144, SAN 20

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over California, near Santa Ana, about 50 min after leaving SFO, or 15 min after leaving SAN. There, 68 of 82 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air.

## Questions

### Is SFO to SAN a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the SFO–SAN route filed 995 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 11 of 995 (1.1%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between SFO and SAN?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over California, near Santa Ana, about 50 min after leaving SFO, or 15 min after leaving SAN.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly SFO to SAN?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in June, July and August and a bigger share in December and March. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is SAN to SFO the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at SFO and SAN?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days). SAN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 20 days in the past year, most often in February (4 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
