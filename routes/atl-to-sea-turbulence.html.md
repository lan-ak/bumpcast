# ATL to SEA turbulence

> Atlanta (ATL) ⇄ Seattle (SEA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Atlanta (ATL) and Seattle (SEA) fly about 2,180 miles, roughly 4½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Tennessee, and more often in May and June.

- Distance: 2,180 miles (3,510 km)
- In the air: about 4 h 30 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 19,207
- Severe reports: 70 (0.4%)
- Gusty days: ATL 65, SEA 48

## Questions

### Is ATL to SEA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ATL–SEA route filed 19,207 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 70 of 19,207 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between ATL and SEA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Tennessee, near Nashville, about 35 min after leaving ATL, or 3 h 50 min after leaving SEA. The next most were over Nebraska, near Omaha, about 1 h 40 min after leaving ATL, or 2 h 45 min after leaving SEA.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ATL to SEA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in September and a bigger share in May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is SEA to ATL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from SEA to ATL usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ATL and SEA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ATL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 65 days in the past year, most often in March (10 days). SEA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 48 days in the past year, most often in December (18 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
