# LGA to BNA turbulence

> New York (LGA) ⇄ Nashville (BNA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between New York (LGA) and Nashville (BNA) fly about 760 miles, roughly 1¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over West Virginia, and more often in December and February.

- Distance: 760 miles (1,230 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 45 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 11,852
- Severe reports: 59 (0.5%)

## Questions

### Is LGA to BNA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LGA–BNA route filed 11,852 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 59 of 11,852 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between LGA and BNA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over West Virginia, about 1 h after leaving LGA, or 45 min after leaving BNA. The next most were over Kentucky, near Knoxville/Maryville, about 1 h 15 min after leaving LGA, or 25 min after leaving BNA.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly LGA to BNA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in August and September and a bigger share in December and February. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is BNA to LGA the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from BNA to LGA usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LGA and BNA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LGA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 146 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days). BNA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 97 days in the past year, most often in March (17 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
