# ATL to BNA turbulence

> Atlanta (ATL) ⇄ Nashville (BNA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Atlanta (ATL) and Nashville (BNA) fly about 210 miles, roughly 45 minutes in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Georgia, and more often in May, June and October.

- Distance: 210 miles (340 km)
- In the air: about 45 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 1,979
- Severe reports: 5 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: ATL 65, BNA 97

## Questions

### Is ATL to BNA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ATL–BNA route filed 1,979 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 5 of 1,979 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between ATL and BNA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Georgia, near Atlanta, about 10 min after leaving ATL, or 25 min after leaving BNA. The next most were over Tennessee, near Nashville, about 25 min after leaving ATL, or 15 min after leaving BNA.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ATL to BNA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in December, August and September and a bigger share in May, June and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is BNA to ATL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
