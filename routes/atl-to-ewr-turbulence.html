Atlanta ⇄ Newark

ATL to EWR turbulence

Flights between Atlanta (ATL) and Newark (EWR) fly about 740 miles, roughly 1¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Virginia, and more often in May and June.

A year of pilot reports

Strong bumps are rare

Only 71 of 12,165 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.6%). Light chop was the level reported most: 4,963 reports.

Takeoff and landing

ATL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 65 days in the past year, most often in March (10 days). EWR had gusts of 25 knots or more on 145 days in the past year, most often in December (19 days). Questions

Is ATL to EWR a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ATL–EWR route filed 12,165 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 71 of 12,165 (0.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between ATL and EWR?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Virginia, near Greensboro, about 45 min after leaving ATL, or 55 min after leaving EWR. The next most were over North Carolina, near Charlotte, about 25 min after leaving ATL, or 1 h 15 min after leaving EWR.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly ATL to EWR?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in March, July and September and a bigger share in May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is EWR to ATL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from ATL to EWR usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ATL and EWR?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ATL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 65 days in the past year, most often in March (10 days). EWR had gusts of 25 knots or more on 145 days in the past year, most often in December (19 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 12,165 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct ATL–EWR path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
