# ATL to TPA turbulence

> Atlanta (ATL) ⇄ Tampa (TPA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Atlanta (ATL) and Tampa (TPA) fly about 410 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Georgia, and more often in November, December and February.

- Distance: 410 miles (660 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 5 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 7,732
- Severe reports: 22 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: ATL 65, TPA 45

## Questions

### Is ATL to TPA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ATL–TPA route filed 7,732 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 22 of 7,732 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between ATL and TPA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Georgia, about 25 min after leaving ATL, or 35 min after leaving TPA. The next most were over Florida, near Jacksonville, about 35 min after leaving ATL, or 25 min after leaving TPA.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ATL to TPA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in November, December and February. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is TPA to ATL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ATL and TPA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ATL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 65 days in the past year, most often in March (10 days). TPA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 45 days in the past year, most often in February (6 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
