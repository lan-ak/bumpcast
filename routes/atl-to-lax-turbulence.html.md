# ATL to LAX turbulence

> Atlanta (ATL) ⇄ Los Angeles (LAX), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Atlanta (ATL) and Los Angeles (LAX) fly about 1,940 miles, roughly 4 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Mississippi.

- Distance: 1,940 miles (3,130 km)
- In the air: about 4 h 5 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 23,347
- Severe reports: 85 (0.4%)

## Questions

### Is ATL to LAX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ATL–LAX route filed 23,347 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 85 of 23,347 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between ATL and LAX?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Mississippi, near Memphis, about 45 min after leaving ATL, or 3 h 15 min after leaving LAX. The next most were over Texas, about 2 h 5 min after leaving ATL, or 1 h 55 min after leaving LAX.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ATL to LAX?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is LAX to ATL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAX to ATL usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ATL and LAX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ATL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 65 days in the past year, most often in March (10 days). LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
