Ottawa ⇄ Newark

YOW to EWR turbulence

Flights between Ottawa (YOW) and Newark (EWR) fly about 330 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over New York, and more often in December and October.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 25 of 5,069 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.5%). Light chop was the level reported most: 2,851 reports.

Takeoff and landing

Questions

Is YOW to EWR a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YOW–EWR route filed 5,069 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 25 of 5,069 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between YOW and EWR?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over New York, near Syracuse, about 25 min after leaving YOW, or 30 min after leaving EWR.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly YOW to EWR?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in April and a bigger share in December and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is EWR to YOW the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YOW and EWR?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. YOW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 79 days in the past year, most often in December (12 days). EWR had gusts of 25 knots or more on 145 days in the past year, most often in December (19 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 5,069 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct YOW–EWR path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
