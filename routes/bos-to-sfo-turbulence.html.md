# BOS to SFO turbulence

> Boston (BOS) ⇄ San Francisco (SFO), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Boston (BOS) and San Francisco (SFO) fly about 2,700 miles, roughly 5½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over New York, and more often in June.

- Distance: 2,700 miles (4,340 km)
- In the air: about 5 h 30 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 30,188
- Severe reports: 94 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: BOS 148, SFO 144

## Questions

### Is BOS to SFO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the BOS–SFO route filed 30,188 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 94 of 30,188 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between BOS and SFO?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over New York, near Syracuse, about 30 min after leaving BOS, or 5 h after leaving SFO.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly BOS to SFO?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in January and a bigger share in June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is SFO to BOS the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from SFO to BOS usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at BOS and SFO?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. BOS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 148 days in the past year, most often in December (23 days). SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
