# LAX to SEA turbulence

> Los Angeles (LAX) ⇄ Seattle (SEA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Los Angeles (LAX) and Seattle (SEA) fly about 960 miles, roughly 2¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near LAX, and more often in December. Severe reports were rare: 17 of 2,883.

- Distance: 960 miles (1,540 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 10 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 2,883
- Severe reports: 17 (0.6%)
- Gusty days: LAX 17, SEA 48

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often near LAX, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving LAX (2 h after leaving SEA): 181 of 305 reports there. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air.

The next most were over California, about 1 h 10 min after leaving LAX (55 min after leaving SEA): 128 of 209 reports there. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height.

## Questions

### Is LAX to SEA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAX–SEA route filed 2,883 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 17 of 2,883 (0.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between LAX and SEA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near LAX, where flights climb out or come in to land (about 5 min after leaving LAX (2 h after leaving SEA)), and next most over California (about 1 h 10 min after leaving LAX (55 min after leaving SEA)).

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAX to SEA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in March and July and a bigger share in December. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is SEA to LAX the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAX and SEA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days). SEA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 48 days in the past year, most often in December (18 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
