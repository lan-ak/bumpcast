LAX to PHX turbulence

Flights between Los Angeles (LAX) and Phoenix (PHX) fly about 370 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over California, and more often in January, March and May. Severe reports were rare: 16 of 629.

Questions

Is LAX to PHX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAX–PHX route filed 629 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 16 of 629 (2.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between LAX and PHX?

Moderate bumps were reported most often over California, near Santa Ana, about 5 min after leaving LAX (55 min after leaving PHX): 53 of 74 reports there. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAX to PHX?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in January, March and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is PHX to LAX the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAX and PHX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days). PHX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 68 days in the past year, most often in June (13 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 629 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct LAX–PHX path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
