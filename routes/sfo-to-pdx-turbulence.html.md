# SFO to PDX turbulence

> San Francisco (SFO) ⇄ Portland (PDX), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between San Francisco (SFO) and Portland (PDX) fly about 550 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Oregon, and more often in November.

- Distance: 550 miles (890 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 20 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 1,053
- Severe reports: 6 (0.6%)
- Gusty days: SFO 144, PDX 40

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Oregon, about 45 min after leaving SFO, or 35 min after leaving PDX. There, 73 of 136 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

The next most were near SFO, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving SFO, or 1 h 20 min after leaving PDX. There, 57 of 107 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at SFO add to it.

## Takeoff and landing

SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days). PDX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 40 days in the past year, most often in December (9 days).

## Questions

### Is SFO to PDX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the SFO–PDX route filed 1,053 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 6 of 1,053 (0.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between SFO and PDX?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Oregon, about 45 min after leaving SFO, or 35 min after leaving PDX. The next most were near SFO, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving SFO, or 1 h 20 min after leaving PDX.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly SFO to PDX?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in March, August and September and a bigger share in November. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is PDX to SFO the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at SFO and PDX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days). PDX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 40 days in the past year, most often in December (9 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
