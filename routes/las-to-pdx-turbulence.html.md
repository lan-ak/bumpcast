# LAS to PDX turbulence

> Las Vegas (LAS) ⇄ Portland (PDX), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Las Vegas (LAS) and Portland (PDX) fly about 760 miles, roughly 1¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over or near Nevada, and more often in December.

- Distance: 760 miles (1,230 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 45 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 1,777
- Severe reports: 5 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: LAS 109, PDX 40

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over or near Nevada, about 30 min after leaving LAS, or 1 h 10 min after leaving PDX. There, 118 of 180 reports were moderate or stronger. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height.

The next most were over Nevada, near Reno, about 45 min after leaving LAS, or 1 h after leaving PDX. There, 96 of 173 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is LAS to PDX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAS–PDX route filed 1,777 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 5 of 1,777 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between LAS and PDX?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over or near Nevada, about 30 min after leaving LAS, or 1 h 10 min after leaving PDX. The next most were over Nevada, near Reno, about 45 min after leaving LAS, or 1 h after leaving PDX.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAS to PDX?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July and August and a bigger share in December. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is PDX to LAS the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAS and PDX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 109 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). PDX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 40 days in the past year, most often in December (9 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
