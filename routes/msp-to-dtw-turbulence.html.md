# MSP to DTW turbulence

> Minneapolis (MSP) ⇄ Detroit (DTW), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Minneapolis (MSP) and Detroit (DTW) fly about 530 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Wisconsin, and more often in April, May and June.

- Distance: 530 miles (850 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 20 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 8,117
- Severe reports: 20 (0.2%)
- Gusty days: MSP 127, DTW 106

## Questions

### Is MSP to DTW a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the MSP–DTW route filed 8,117 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 20 of 8,117 (0.2%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between MSP and DTW?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Wisconsin, near Milwaukee, about 40 min after leaving MSP, or 35 min after leaving DTW. The next most were over Michigan, near Grand Rapids, about 55 min after leaving MSP, or 20 min after leaving DTW.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly MSP to DTW?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in January, February and October and a bigger share in April, May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is DTW to MSP the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from MSP to DTW usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at MSP and DTW?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. MSP had gusts of 25 knots or more on 127 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). DTW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 106 days in the past year, most often in January (14 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
