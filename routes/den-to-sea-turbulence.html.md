# DEN to SEA turbulence

> Denver (DEN) ⇄ Seattle (SEA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Denver (DEN) and Seattle (SEA) fly about 1,020 miles, roughly 2¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, and more often in December. Severe reports were rare: 83 of 11,594.

- Distance: 1,020 miles (1,640 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 15 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 11,594
- Severe reports: 83 (0.7%)
- Gusty days: DEN 155, SEA 48

## Questions

### Is DEN to SEA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DEN–SEA route filed 11,594 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 83 of 11,594 (0.7%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between DEN and SEA?

Moderate bumps were reported most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving DEN (2 h 10 min after leaving SEA): 1,388 of 4,792 reports there. Close to the ground, wind over the mountains stirs the air, and gusty days at DEN add to it.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly DEN to SEA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July and October and a bigger share in December. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is SEA to DEN the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from SEA to DEN usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DEN and SEA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). SEA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 48 days in the past year, most often in December (18 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
