# LAX to DTW turbulence

> Los Angeles (LAX) ⇄ Detroit (DTW), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Los Angeles (LAX) and Detroit (DTW) fly about 1,970 miles, roughly 4¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, and more often in May and June.

- Distance: 1,970 miles (3,180 km)
- In the air: about 4 h 10 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 29,401
- Severe reports: 88 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: LAX 17, DTW 106

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Colorado, near Colorado Springs, about 1 h 45 min after leaving LAX, or 2 h 20 min after leaving DTW. There, 895 of 2,472 reports were moderate or stronger. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height.

The next most were over Michigan, near Grand Rapids, about 3 h 40 min after leaving LAX, or 20 min after leaving DTW. There, 639 of 2,706 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is LAX to DTW a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAX–DTW route filed 29,401 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 88 of 29,401 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between LAX and DTW?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, near Colorado Springs, about 1 h 45 min after leaving LAX, or 2 h 20 min after leaving DTW. The next most were over Michigan, near Grand Rapids, about 3 h 40 min after leaving LAX, or 20 min after leaving DTW.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAX to DTW?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in April, July and August and a bigger share in May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is DTW to LAX the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAX to DTW usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAX and DTW?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days). DTW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 106 days in the past year, most often in January (14 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
