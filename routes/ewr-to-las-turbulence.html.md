# EWR to LAS turbulence

> Newark (EWR) ⇄ Las Vegas (LAS), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Newark (EWR) and Las Vegas (LAS) fly about 2,220 miles, roughly 4½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Ohio, and more often in May.

- Distance: 2,220 miles (3,580 km)
- In the air: about 4 h 35 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 34,218
- Severe reports: 104 (0.3%)

## Questions

### Is EWR to LAS a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the EWR–LAS route filed 34,218 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 104 of 34,218 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between EWR and LAS?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Ohio, near Cleveland, about 50 min after leaving EWR, or 3 h 40 min after leaving LAS. The next most were over Illinois, near Chicago, about 1 h 30 min after leaving EWR, or 3 h after leaving LAS.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly EWR to LAS?

Moderate bumps made up a bigger share of reports in May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is LAS to EWR the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAS to EWR usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at EWR and LAS?

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
