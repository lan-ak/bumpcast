# MSP to DFW turbulence

> Minneapolis (MSP) ⇄ Dallas-Fort Worth (DFW), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Minneapolis (MSP) and Dallas-Fort Worth (DFW) fly about 850 miles, roughly 2 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Iowa, and more often in April and June.

- Distance: 850 miles (1,370 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 55 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 10,238
- Severe reports: 48 (0.5%)
- Gusty days: MSP 127, DFW 122

## Questions

### Is MSP to DFW a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the MSP–DFW route filed 10,238 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 48 of 10,238 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between MSP and DFW?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Iowa, near Des Moines, about 25 min after leaving MSP, or 1 h 30 min after leaving DFW. The next most were over Oklahoma, near Oklahoma City, about 1 h 30 min after leaving MSP, or 25 min after leaving DFW.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly MSP to DFW?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in November and August and a bigger share in April and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is DFW to MSP the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at MSP and DFW?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. MSP had gusts of 25 knots or more on 127 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). DFW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 122 days in the past year, most often in March (21 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
