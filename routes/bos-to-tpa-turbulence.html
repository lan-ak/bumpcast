Boston ⇄ Tampa

BOS to TPA turbulence

Flights between Boston (BOS) and Tampa (TPA) fly about 1,190 miles, roughly 2½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Virginia, and more often in November, December and February.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 80 of 13,058 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.6%). Light chop was the level reported most: 6,049 reports.

Takeoff and landing

BOS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 148 days in the past year, most often in December (23 days). TPA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 45 days in the past year, most often in February (6 days). Questions

Is BOS to TPA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the BOS–TPA route filed 13,058 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 80 of 13,058 (0.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between BOS and TPA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Virginia, near Norfolk, about 1 h after leaving BOS, or 1 h 35 min after leaving TPA.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly BOS to TPA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in November, December and February. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is TPA to BOS the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from TPA to BOS usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at BOS and TPA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. BOS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 148 days in the past year, most often in December (23 days). TPA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 45 days in the past year, most often in February (6 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 13,058 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct BOS–TPA path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
