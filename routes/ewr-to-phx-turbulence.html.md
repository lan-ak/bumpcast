# EWR to PHX turbulence

> Newark (EWR) ⇄ Phoenix (PHX), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Newark (EWR) and Phoenix (PHX) fly about 2,130 miles, roughly 4½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Indiana.

## Questions

### Is EWR to PHX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the EWR–PHX route filed 29,603 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 113 of 29,603 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between EWR and PHX?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Indiana, near Indianapolis, about 1 h 25 min after leaving EWR, or 2 h 55 min after leaving PHX. The next most were over Pennsylvania, near Pittsburgh, about 35 min after leaving EWR, or 3 h 45 min after leaving PHX.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly EWR to PHX?

Moderate bumps made up a similar share of reports all year. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is PHX to EWR the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from PHX to EWR usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at EWR and PHX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. EWR had gusts of 25 knots or more on 145 days in the past year, most often in December (19 days). PHX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 68 days in the past year, most often in June (13 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
