Las Vegas ⇄ Houston

LAS to IAH turbulence

Flights between Las Vegas (LAS) and Houston (IAH) fly about 1,220 miles, roughly 2¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Texas, and more often in February, March and May.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 29 of 8,828 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.3%). Light chop was the level reported most: 4,215 reports.

Questions

Is LAS to IAH a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAS–IAH route filed 8,828 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 29 of 8,828 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between LAS and IAH?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Texas, near Austin, about 2 h 10 min after leaving LAS, or 25 min after leaving IAH.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAS to IAH?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in November, July and August and a bigger share in February, March and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is IAH to LAS the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAS to IAH usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAS and IAH?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 109 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). IAH had gusts of 25 knots or more on 62 days in the past year, most often in March (12 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 8,828 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct LAS–IAH path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
