Chicago ⇄ San Francisco

ORD to SFO turbulence

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and San Francisco (SFO) fly about 1,840 miles, roughly 3¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, and more often in May and October.

A year of pilot reports

Strong bumps are rare

Only 95 of 24,381 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.4%). Light chop was the level reported most: 11,220 reports.

Takeoff and landing

ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days). Questions

Is ORD to SFO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–SFO route filed 24,381 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 95 of 24,381 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between ORD and SFO?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 10 min after leaving ORD, or 3 h 40 min after leaving SFO. The next most were over Iowa, near Des Moines, about 45 min after leaving ORD, or 3 h 5 min after leaving SFO.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to SFO?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July and August and a bigger share in May and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is SFO to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from SFO to ORD usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and SFO?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 24,381 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct ORD–SFO path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
