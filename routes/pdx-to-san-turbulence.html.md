# PDX to SAN turbulence

> Portland (PDX) ⇄ San Diego (SAN), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Portland (PDX) and San Diego (SAN) fly about 930 miles, roughly 2 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Oregon, and more often in April.

- Distance: 930 miles (1,500 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 5 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 2,448
- Severe reports: 26 (1.1%)
- Gusty days: PDX 40, SAN 20

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Oregon, about 35 min after leaving PDX, or 1 h 25 min after leaving SAN. There, 160 of 299 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

The next most were over California, near Santa Ana, about 1 h 50 min after leaving PDX, or 15 min after leaving SAN. There, 122 of 147 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air.

## Questions

### Is PDX to SAN a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the PDX–SAN route filed 2,448 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 26 of 2,448 (1.1%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between PDX and SAN?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Oregon, about 35 min after leaving PDX, or 1 h 25 min after leaving SAN. The next most were over California, near Santa Ana, about 1 h 50 min after leaving PDX, or 15 min after leaving SAN.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly PDX to SAN?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in March and July and a bigger share in April. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is SAN to PDX the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at PDX and SAN?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. PDX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 40 days in the past year, most often in December (9 days). SAN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 20 days in the past year, most often in February (4 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
