# DEN to MSP turbulence

> Denver (DEN) ⇄ Minneapolis (MSP), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Denver (DEN) and Minneapolis (MSP) fly about 680 miles, roughly 1½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, and more often in June.

- Distance: 680 miles (1,090 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 35 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 11,138
- Severe reports: 52 (0.5%)
- Gusty days: DEN 155, MSP 127

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving DEN, or 1 h 30 min after leaving MSP. There, 851 of 3,811 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over the mountains stirs the air, and gusty days at DEN add to it.

## Takeoff and landing

DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). MSP had gusts of 25 knots or more on 127 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days).

## Questions

### Is DEN to MSP a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DEN–MSP route filed 11,138 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 52 of 11,138 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between DEN and MSP?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving DEN, or 1 h 30 min after leaving MSP.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly DEN to MSP?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in September and a bigger share in June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is MSP to DEN the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from DEN to MSP usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DEN and MSP?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). MSP had gusts of 25 knots or more on 127 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
