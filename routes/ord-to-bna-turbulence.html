Chicago ⇄ Nashville

ORD to BNA turbulence

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and Nashville (BNA) fly about 410 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Illinois, and more often in April.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 42 of 11,032 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.4%). Light chop was the level reported most: 5,300 reports.

Questions

Is ORD to BNA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–BNA route filed 11,032 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 42 of 11,032 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between ORD and BNA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Illinois, near Chicago, about 5 min after leaving ORD, or 55 min after leaving BNA. The next most were over Indiana, near Indianapolis, about 25 min after leaving ORD, or 35 min after leaving BNA.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to BNA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July and September and a bigger share in April. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is BNA to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and BNA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). BNA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 97 days in the past year, most often in March (17 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 11,032 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct ORD–BNA path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
