# EWR to LAX turbulence

> Newark (EWR) ⇄ Los Angeles (LAX), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Newark (EWR) and Los Angeles (LAX) fly about 2,450 miles, roughly 5 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Ohio.

- Distance: 2,450 miles (3,940 km)
- In the air: about 5 h 5 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 34,865
- Severe reports: 126 (0.4%)
- Gusty days: EWR 145, LAX 17

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Ohio, about 1 h 10 min after leaving EWR, or 3 h 50 min after leaving LAX. There, 1,057 of 4,791 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

The next most were over Colorado, near Colorado Springs, about 3 h 20 min after leaving EWR, or 1 h 40 min after leaving LAX. There, 1,022 of 2,862 reports were moderate or stronger. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height.

## Questions

### Is EWR to LAX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the EWR–LAX route filed 34,865 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 126 of 34,865 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between EWR and LAX?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Ohio, about 1 h 10 min after leaving EWR, or 3 h 50 min after leaving LAX. The next most were over Colorado, near Colorado Springs, about 3 h 20 min after leaving EWR, or 1 h 40 min after leaving LAX.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly EWR to LAX?

Moderate bumps made up a similar share of reports all year. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is LAX to EWR the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAX to EWR usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at EWR and LAX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. EWR had gusts of 25 knots or more on 145 days in the past year, most often in December (19 days). LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
