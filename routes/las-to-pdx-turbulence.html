Las Vegas ⇄ Portland

LAS to PDX turbulence

Flights between Las Vegas (LAS) and Portland (PDX) fly about 760 miles, roughly 1¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over or near Nevada, and more often in December.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 5 of 1,777 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.3%). Moderate was the level reported most: 1,000 reports.

Takeoff and landing

LAS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 109 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). PDX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 40 days in the past year, most often in December (9 days). Questions

Is LAS to PDX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAS–PDX route filed 1,777 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 5 of 1,777 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between LAS and PDX?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over or near Nevada, about 30 min after leaving LAS, or 1 h 10 min after leaving PDX. The next most were over Nevada, near Reno, about 45 min after leaving LAS, or 1 h after leaving PDX.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAS to PDX?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July and August and a bigger share in December. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is PDX to LAS the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAS and PDX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 109 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). PDX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 40 days in the past year, most often in December (9 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 1,777 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct LAS–PDX path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
