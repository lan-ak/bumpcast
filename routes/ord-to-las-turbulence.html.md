# ORD to LAS turbulence

> Chicago (ORD) ⇄ Las Vegas (LAS), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and Las Vegas (LAS) fly about 1,510 miles, roughly 3¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, and more often in May, June and October.

- Distance: 1,510 miles (2,430 km)
- In the air: about 3 h 15 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 27,216
- Severe reports: 92 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: ORD 123, LAS 109

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Colorado, near Colorado Springs, about 1 h 55 min after leaving ORD, or 1 h 10 min after leaving LAS. There, 677 of 2,045 reports were moderate or stronger. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height.

The next most were near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 10 min after leaving ORD, or 3 h 5 min after leaving LAS. There, 544 of 2,723 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at ORD add to it.

## Questions

### Is ORD to LAS a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–LAS route filed 27,216 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 92 of 27,216 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between ORD and LAS?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, near Colorado Springs, about 1 h 55 min after leaving ORD, or 1 h 10 min after leaving LAS. The next most were near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 10 min after leaving ORD, or 3 h 5 min after leaving LAS.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to LAS?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July and August and a bigger share in May, June and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is LAS to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAS to ORD usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and LAS?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). LAS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 109 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
