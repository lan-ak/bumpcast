Los Angeles ⇄ Miami

LAX to MIA turbulence

Flights between Los Angeles (LAX) and Miami (MIA) fly about 2,340 miles, roughly 4¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Texas, and more often in December, February and April.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 65 of 19,149 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.3%). Light chop was the level reported most: 8,197 reports.

Takeoff and landing

LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days). MIA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 58 days in the past year, most often in April (9 days). Questions

Is LAX to MIA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAX–MIA route filed 19,149 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 65 of 19,149 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between LAX and MIA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Texas, near Austin, about 2 h 30 min after leaving LAX, or 2 h 15 min after leaving MIA.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAX to MIA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in December, February and April. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is MIA to LAX the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAX to MIA usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAX and MIA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days). MIA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 58 days in the past year, most often in April (9 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 19,149 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct LAX–MIA path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
