Toronto ⇄ Boston

YYZ to BOS turbulence

Flights between Toronto (YYZ) and Boston (BOS) fly about 440 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over New York, and more often in December, June and October.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 31 of 7,847 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.4%). Light chop was the level reported most: 4,508 reports.

Questions

Is YYZ to BOS a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YYZ–BOS route filed 7,847 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 31 of 7,847 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between YYZ and BOS?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over New York, near Syracuse, about 30 min after leaving YYZ, or 35 min after leaving BOS.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly YYZ to BOS?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in November and January and a bigger share in December, June and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is BOS to YYZ the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from YYZ to BOS usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YYZ and BOS?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. YYZ had gusts of 25 knots or more on 120 days in the past year, most often in January (18 days). BOS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 148 days in the past year, most often in December (23 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 7,847 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct YYZ–BOS path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
