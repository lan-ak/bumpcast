# PHL to FLL turbulence

> Philadelphia (PHL) ⇄ Fort Lauderdale (FLL), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Philadelphia (PHL) and Fort Lauderdale (FLL) fly about 990 miles, roughly 2¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Virginia, and more often in November, December and February.

- Distance: 990 miles (1,600 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 15 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 9,981
- Severe reports: 82 (0.8%)
- Gusty days: PHL 118, FLL 68

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Virginia, near Norfolk, about 30 min after leaving PHL, or 1 h 35 min after leaving FLL. There, 521 of 791 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is PHL to FLL a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the PHL–FLL route filed 9,981 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 82 of 9,981 (0.8%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between PHL and FLL?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Virginia, near Norfolk, about 30 min after leaving PHL, or 1 h 35 min after leaving FLL.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly PHL to FLL?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in November, December and February. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is FLL to PHL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at PHL and FLL?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. PHL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 118 days in the past year, most often in May (17 days). FLL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 68 days in the past year, most often in April (13 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
