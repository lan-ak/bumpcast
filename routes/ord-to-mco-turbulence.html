Chicago ⇄ Orlando

ORD to MCO turbulence

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and Orlando (MCO) fly about 1,010 miles, roughly 2¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, and more often in February, April and May.

A year of pilot reports

Strong bumps are rare

Only 80 of 30,664 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.3%). Light chop was the level reported most: 15,235 reports.

Takeoff and landing

ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). MCO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 61 days in the past year, most often in October (9 days). Questions

Is ORD to MCO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–MCO route filed 30,664 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 80 of 30,664 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between ORD and MCO?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving ORD, or 2 h 10 min after leaving MCO. The next most were over Indiana, near Indianapolis, about 25 min after leaving ORD, or 1 h 45 min after leaving MCO.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to MCO?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in February, April and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is MCO to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and MCO?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). MCO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 61 days in the past year, most often in October (9 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 30,664 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct ORD–MCO path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
