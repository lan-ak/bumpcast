JFK to LAX turbulence

Flights between New York (JFK) and Los Angeles (LAX) fly about 2,470 miles, roughly 5 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado. Severe reports were rare: 120 of 35,528.

Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Colorado, near Colorado Springs, about 3 h 20 min after leaving JFK (1 h 40 min after leaving LAX): 1,072 of 2,964 reports there. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height. More on mountain waves.

The next most were over Pennsylvania, near Pittsburgh, about 40 min after leaving JFK (4 h 20 min after leaving LAX): 1,056 of 1,571 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms. More on clear-air turbulence and turbulence near clouds.

Questions

Is JFK to LAX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the JFK–LAX route filed 35,528 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 120 of 35,528 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between JFK and LAX?

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Colorado, near Colorado Springs, about 3 h 20 min after leaving JFK (1 h 40 min after leaving LAX): 1,072 of 2,964 reports there. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height. The next most were over Pennsylvania, near Pittsburgh, about 40 min after leaving JFK (4 h 20 min after leaving LAX): 1,056 of 1,571 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly JFK to LAX?

Moderate bumps made up a similar share of reports all year. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is LAX to JFK the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAX to JFK usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at JFK and LAX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. JFK had gusts of 25 knots or more on 157 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days). LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 35,528 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct JFK–LAX path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
