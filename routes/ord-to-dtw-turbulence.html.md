# ORD to DTW turbulence

> Chicago (ORD) ⇄ Detroit (DTW), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and Detroit (DTW) fly about 230 miles, roughly 45 minutes in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Michigan, and more often in March, April and May.

- Distance: 230 miles (380 km)
- In the air: about 45 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 5,874
- Severe reports: 35 (0.6%)
- Gusty days: ORD 123, DTW 106

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Michigan, near Grand Rapids, about 20 min after leaving ORD, or 20 min after leaving DTW. There, 198 of 949 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

The next most were near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 0 min after leaving ORD, or 45 min after leaving DTW. There, 144 of 1,076 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at ORD add to it.

## Questions

### Is ORD to DTW a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–DTW route filed 5,874 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 35 of 5,874 (0.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between ORD and DTW?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Michigan, near Grand Rapids, about 20 min after leaving ORD, or 20 min after leaving DTW. The next most were near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 0 min after leaving ORD, or 45 min after leaving DTW.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to DTW?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in December, January and September and a bigger share in March, April and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is DTW to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and DTW?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). DTW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 106 days in the past year, most often in January (14 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
