# YVR to YYZ turbulence

> Vancouver (YVR) ⇄ Toronto (YYZ), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Vancouver (YVR) and Toronto (YYZ) fly about 2,080 miles, roughly 4¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Michigan, and more often in June and August. Severe reports were rare: 21 of 3,769.

- Distance: 2,080 miles (3,350 km)
- In the air: about 4 h 20 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 3,769
- Severe reports: 21 (0.6%)
- Gusty days: YVR 32, YYZ 120

## Questions

### Is YVR to YYZ a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YVR–YYZ route filed 3,769 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 21 of 3,769 (0.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. Moderate bumps were reported most often over Michigan, about 3 h 40 min after leaving YVR (35 min after leaving YYZ): 164 of 600 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly YVR to YYZ?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in January and February and a bigger share in June and August. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is YYZ to YVR the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from YVR to YYZ usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YVR and YYZ?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. YVR had gusts of 25 knots or more on 32 days in the past year, most often in December (8 days). YYZ had gusts of 25 knots or more on 120 days in the past year, most often in January (18 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
