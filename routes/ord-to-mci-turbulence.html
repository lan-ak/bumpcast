Chicago ⇄ Kansas City

ORD to MCI turbulence

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and Kansas City (MCI) fly about 400 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, and more often in March and May.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 30 of 5,638 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.5%). Light chop was the level reported most: 2,428 reports.

Takeoff and landing

ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). MCI had gusts of 25 knots or more on 115 days in the past year, most often in March (20 days). Questions

Is ORD to MCI a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–MCI route filed 5,638 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 30 of 5,638 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between ORD and MCI?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving ORD, or 1 h after leaving MCI.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to MCI?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, September and October and a bigger share in March and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is MCI to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and MCI?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). MCI had gusts of 25 knots or more on 115 days in the past year, most often in March (20 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 5,638 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct ORD–MCI path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
