## Rules for every video (paste at the end of each brief)

**Always**
- Say and show the name: **BumpCast**, one word. Put it on screen and in the caption, so people can search for it.
- Disclose it: on TikTok, turn on Content disclosure → Your brand (the account is BumpCast's), and add #ad to every caption on both apps.
- Use real forecasts from the app, from your own flight or another real upcoming flight. Screen-record them yourself.
- Keep it calm. BumpCast's whole point is that turbulence is normal and can be forecast. Funny and honest are great. Scary isn't.

**Never**
- Say BumpCast keeps you safe, predicts every bump or is always right. It's for comfort and planning.
- Say it treats or cures anxiety or fear of flying. Your own experience ("it helps me feel calmer") is fine if it's true.
- Use scary edits: screams, alarm sounds, crash or emergency footage, red flashing text.
- Use footage of real incidents or injured people.
- Fake or exaggerate turbulence, or film on the ground as if you were in the air.
- Film flight attendants or other passengers' faces.
- Unbuckle, stand up or ignore the crew to get a shot.
- Mention or criticize other apps.

**Things you can say about BumpCast**
- "It forecasts turbulence along your exact route, minute by minute."
- "It tells you when the bumps start, how strong they'll be and when they end."
- "The forecast comes 48 hours before departure, and it updates 24, 6 and 2 hours out."
- "It works in airplane mode, because the forecast is saved on your phone before you board."
- "There's a Lock Screen countdown to the next bumpy stretch."
- "It has guided breathing timed to the next bumpy stretch."
- "It's free to try, for flights in the continental US and southern Canada."

## Brief 1: Talk about it (everyone) (paste)

**The idea:** talk about flying as a nervous flyer: what helps you, what you do the night before, what you wish you'd known. BumpCast comes up as part of your routine, not as the headline. These posts build the account's audience, so they don't need to sell.

**Format:** a TikTok photo slideshow (5 to 7 slides) or a 15 to 30 second talking-head video. Switch between the two. On Instagram, post a slideshow as a Reel (the photos set to music), not a carousel, because carousels don't count views.

**Pick an angle each day**
- **Tips:** your honest nervous-flyer tips, with BumpCast as one of them.
- **The night before:** open BumpCast, type the flight number, and show the grade and timeline. Say what you see ("A few bumps around 40 minutes in, then smooth to landing") and that it updates 24, 6 and 2 hours out, so you check again at the airport.
- **Flying home for the holidays (Nov 10 to 25):** the night-before routine, for everyone flying home for Thanksgiving on Nov 26. Canadian creators: say "flying home for the holidays" instead. We'll run it again for December.
- **Turbulence in the news (only when we message you):** see below.

**Slideshow structure**
1. Slide 1: the hook in big text over a photo (a window seat, the airport, your face).
2. Slides 2 to 5: one tip or step per slide, in a short line.
3. One of those slides is BumpCast: a screenshot of a real forecast, with a line like "I check how bumpy my flight will be the night before."
4. Last slide: a calm closer, like "Save this for your next flight."

**Tips you can use (only the ones that are true for you)**
- Sit over the wing. It's the steadiest part of the plane.
- Check the turbulence forecast the night before, so nothing's a surprise. (This is the BumpCast slide.)
- Noise-cancelling headphones and a downloaded playlist.
- Tell the flight attendant you're nervous.
- Box breathing during takeoff: in for 4, hold for 4, out for 4, hold for 4.
- Know what bumps are: the most common ones at cruise come from fast winds next to slower air, and planes are built for far more than that.
- Keep your seatbelt on whenever you're seated.

**Hooks**
- "Things that actually helped my fear of flying"
- "Nervous flyer starter pack"
- "My pre-flight routine as someone who hates turbulence"
- "POV: you're a nervous flyer with a flight tomorrow"
- "Flying home for Thanksgiving and dreading the turbulence? Do this the night before."
- "Me checking whether my Thanksgiving flight will be bumpy"

**Caption:** "Save this for your next flight. [One line in your own words.] #nervousflyer #fearofflying #flyinganxiety #traveltips #ad". For holiday posts: "Flying for Thanksgiving? Check your flight on BumpCast the night before. #thanksgivingtravel #holidaytravel #nervousflyer #ad"

**Getting the screenshot:** add any real flight leaving in the next 48 hours (yours, or one on a route you fly) and screenshot its forecast.

**When turbulence is in the news**
When a turbulence story goes viral, nervous flyers go looking for answers. We'll message you the story link and the facts, and a calm video in the first 24 hours reaches them while they're looking.
1. Hook: "You've probably seen the turbulence story. As a nervous flyer, here's what helps me." Film it as a talking head over a green-screen of the headline, or stitch a news clip that doesn't show anyone hurt.
2. One or two calm facts, from our message or the list below. Don't guess at causes, and don't add details that weren't reported.
3. "Turbulence is weather, and weather can be forecast. I check my flights on BumpCast before I fly: it shows when the bumps come and how strong they'll be."
4. "And keep your seatbelt on whenever you're seated."

Calm facts you can use:
- The most common bumps at cruise come from fast winds next to slower air, and planes are built for far more than that.
- Bumps over mountains follow the terrain and usually pass once you're beyond them. Crews plan their altitudes around them.
- Crews ask you to keep your seatbelt fastened whenever you're seated, and that's the best protection there is.

In news videos, never say BumpCast would have predicted that flight, show injured people or damage inside the cabin, joke about the incident, or speculate about the cause or blame the airline.

## Brief 2: On the flight (flyers) (paste)

**The idea:** BumpCast told you when the bumps would come. Show the forecast, then show the moment it happened.

**When:** one video per flight. Post it on the flight day if you can, or within 3 days of landing. That also covers a flight that comes before your account is warmed up.

**Before you fly**
- Add your flight in BumpCast as soon as you're hired. The forecast appears 48 hours before departure, so for a flight in the next two days it's already there.
- The night before, screen-record the forecast: the grade (for example, "A few bumps"), then scroll the timeline to the bumpy stretch. Hired the day before you fly? Record it at the gate instead.
- Check that Live Activities are on for BumpCast (Settings → BumpCast → Live Activities), so the countdown shows on your Lock Screen.

**On the flight (seatbelt on the whole time)**
- After takeoff, if the countdown hasn't started, tap "We've taken off" in the app.
- Film your Lock Screen when it shows the next stretch, like "Next: light chop in about 6 min".
- When the stretch arrives, film something calm that shows it: a drink on the tray table, the wing out the window, the seatbelt sign coming on.
- Film again once it's smooth.
- After landing, rate the flight in the app. It asks how bumpy it really was.

**Shot list (20 to 40 seconds)**
1. 0 to 2 seconds: hook text on screen, over you or the window.
2. The forecast screen recording: grade, then timeline.
3. The Lock Screen countdown in the air.
4. The bumps (drink, wing or seatbelt sign), with the time on screen.
5. Smooth again, plus your reaction.
6. Optional: the rating screen after landing.

**Hooks (pick one or write your own)**
- "This app told me exactly when the turbulence would start."
- "It said light chop in 6 minutes. Watch."
- "Nervous flyer test: does a turbulence forecast actually work?"
- "I knew the bumps were coming [X] minutes before they did."
- "My flight home: forecast vs. reality."

**Caption:** "Checked my flight on BumpCast before boarding. It said [grade], with bumps around [time]. [What happened.] #nervousflyer #fearofflying #turbulence #ad"

**If the forecast was off:** don't post the comparison, and don't fake it. Rate the flight in the app, post a Talk about it video that day instead, and let us know how it went.
