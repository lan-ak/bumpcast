Gusty at the airport: one line that says why

When the airport's own forecast expects gusts within an hour of takeoff or landing, a light climb or descent stretch that peaks near the ground gets one plain line saying why. The grade, the stretch count and every push stay the same. It happens on about 1 in 5 flights between windy hubs, and more often in winter, so it's designed as a normal state rather than an alert. The line is for Pro or a flight pass. The new Learn page is free.

This uses the existing explainer template, and it's free for everyone. The diagram has simple teal lines over muted ground, a small level plane, and soft curved wind lines with no jagged lines. This page has no "What will light chop feel like?" haptic button.

Same title, with the cause in the body

This is a local notification, so it works offline in the air. The title is unchanged and gives the level. The body uses forecast tense ("was expected"), because the line comes from the saved forecast.

4 · Every state

When the line shows, and when nothing changes

StateWhat shows
Calm at both ends (most flights)Nothing new
Advance forecast (18–48 h out), or the airport forecast doesn't reach takeoff yetNothing new
Gusty at departure, light climb stretch near the groundThe climb row gets the cause line. If it's the main stretch, the link opens "Gusty at the airport"
Gusty at arrival, light descent stretch near the groundThe descent row gets the cause line
Gusty at both endsTwo rows, each with its own line. The grade is unchanged
Moderate or stronger near the ground, and gustySeverity and "feels like" unchanged, plus the short line. The link stays "Near the ground"
Gusty, but the stretch peaks above about 4,000 ft, or no stretch near the groundNothing new
Wind eases on an updateThe line disappears in place. No push
In the air or offlineThe line is part of the saved forecast, and the reminder uses forecast tense. Freshness comes from the existing footer
Free userNo change, apart from the free Learn page. No blurred line, no teaser

VoiceOver reads the descent row in order: the time, the severity, the "feels like" line, then the whole cause line, including "Normal on a windy day, and the crew plans for it."

Lanre's decisions (Oct 27)
  1. Threshold: gusts of 25 kt or more within an hour of takeoff or landing.
  2. Go-arounds: not mentioned. The explainer doesn't pre-brief them.
