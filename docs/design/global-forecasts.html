Forecasts beyond the US, mixed by place

International routes now get a forecast. One route can mix forecast kinds by place as well as by time: JFK → LHR is confirmed for the first 41 min over the US, then an outlook over the Atlantic. These screens show how each state reads, how the chart shows which stretches are confirmed, and how a recent pilot report is shown. Copy and rules are in docs/design/global-forecasts-copy.md. 2 · Forecast screen

Fade the outlook stretches

The chart fades only outlook stretches, wherever they fall. A thin coverage strip under the time axis shows confirmed (solid) and outlook (dotted). Official advisories still raise a stretch, but there's no extra marker for them: the stretch simply shows its level, which keeps the chart clean. Only a recent pilot report gets a small flag, as it does on the map. Outlook stretches are capped at moderate. A stretch shows strong only when an advisory or a pilot report points to it, and then it says "possible".

3 · Explanation sheets

Plain words about what each forecast is

Tap the badge or the coverage strip to open the state's sheet, or tap a pilot-report flag for its sheet. The agreement row only appears once the backend sends agreement.

4 · Flights list, adding a flight, the waitlist

International routes just work

The add-flight sheet stops sending international routes to the waitlist; they save and get a Global outlook. What's left of the waitlist is only routes the worker still can't forecast, such as polar ones, and it comes from the worker's status rather than the US box in Coverage.swift. People already on the waitlist get one push when their route is covered.
