BumpCast · design exploration · Oct 2026

Calm air, shown honestly

Three visual directions for BumpCast, plus a motion, haptics and iOS 27 plan to build on once the forecast, Live Activity, map and calm tools are working. The audience is nervous and curious flyers. Each screen should lower someone's heart rate, be honest about how sure the forecast is, and teach one thing.

Foundation for every direction

A severity scale that never shouts

The current palette ends in system red, which reads as danger. Proposed: a cool-to-warm ramp ending in a muted plum, always paired with the "your drink" description and a glyph, so color is never the only signal. The thresholds come straight from the worker's EDR cut-offs.

SmoothYour drink stays still.EDR < 0.10
Light chopYour drink ripples.0.10–0.22
ModerateDrinks can spill. Stay seated.0.22–0.34
StrongRare. Belt keeps you safe.≥ 0.34

Idea: a small "water glass" glyph whose surface ripples more at each level becomes the app's signature icon set, used in the timeline, widgets and Dynamic Island. It ties the copy you already wrote to a visual.

Recommendation

Blend them in layers

Apple's iOS 27 brand guidance (WWDC26 session 251) says to keep the Liquid Glass UI layer native and put brand expression in the content layer. That suggests one direction per layer:

Mood from Cabin Dawn

Time-of-day sky backgrounds, generous type, the wave timeline on the summary card, breathing tools.

Proof from Flight Deck

Confidence badge and update time on every forecast, with no data-source names. Tabular numbers. The precise bar chart one tap down for people who want it.

Depth from Field Guide

One "why" card per bumpy stretch, plus a learn library. Start with generic explainers; add per-segment causes once the worker can classify them.

Motion

Transitions that slow things down

Rule of thumb: no bounce, no shake, nothing faster than 0.35 s. Use critically damped springs (.smooth, around 0.5–0.7 s). With Reduce Motion on, every transition becomes a cross-fade.

Haptics

Touch that steadies, never alarms

Haptics should feel like a hand on the shoulder. Keep them soft, sparse and optional, and never use the warning or error patterns for turbulence.

Platform features worth building on

Dynamic Island in landscape

Live Activities essentials · session 223

In iOS 27 the compact and minimal Dynamic Island views also show in landscape, which is how people watch films on a plane. Show "Smooth for 40 min" next to the movie.

Check isDynamicIslandLimitedInWidth and switch to a narrower glass glyph plus countdown.

Apple Watch Smart Stack and CarPlay

Session 223 · small activity family

A glance at the wrist mid-flight beats unlocking a phone. CarPlay can show the forecast on the drive to the airport.

Add .supplementalActivityFamilies([.small]) with its own layout.

Buttons on the Live Activity

Session 223 · LiveActivityIntent

"Breathe" starts the calm tool from the Lock Screen. After landing, "How did it feel?" collects the rating your README already needs to calibrate the advance forecast.

Refreshed Liquid Glass

What's new in SwiftUI · session 269

Rebuilding against the iOS 27 SDK picks up the new glass, which also follows the system tint slider. Keep tab bars and toolbars standard.

Use toolbarMinimizeBehavior(.onScrollDown) on the long forecast screen and Tab(role: .prominent) for the Calm tab.

On-device explainer that works offline

Foundation Models · sessions 241, 242

The rebuilt on-device model runs in airplane mode. Answer "Is this bump normal?" using only the stored forecast and your explainer library, passed in through tool calling.

Guardrail: the model explains; it never predicts or changes a forecast.

Siri and Spotlight

App Schemas · sessions 240, 343

"How bumpy is my flight to LA?" Make each flight an IndexedEntity, and annotate the forecast screen so Siri can talk about what's on it.

StandBy at the gate

Session 223

Charging at the gate in StandBy, the Live Activity scales up. Use activityBackgroundTint for an edge-to-edge night-sky tint.

Accessible timeline

Custom controls · session 220

Make the timeline an adjustable control: VoiceOver swipes move through the stretches and read "3 h 40 min in, light chop for 8 minutes". Add a Swift Charts audio graph as well.

Ground rules

Keeping it calm and credible

Do

  • Show the confidence label and update time on every forecast
  • Say what you don't know: advance forecast, out of coverage
  • Lead with duration and timing ("8 min, 3 h in")
  • Pair every color with a word and a glyph
  • Support Dynamic Type up to accessibility sizes
  • Explain what the crew is doing during each stretch

Don't

  • Use red, warning icons or alarm haptics
  • Name data sources anywhere in the app or notifications
  • Show raw EDR on the main screen
  • Animate with bounce, shake or flashing
  • Push notifications at night for small changes
  • Imply certainty the data doesn't support
  • Hide the "why"; that's what turns fear into curiosity
