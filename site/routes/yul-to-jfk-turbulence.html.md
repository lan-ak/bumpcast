# YUL to JFK turbulence

> Montréal (YUL) ⇄ New York (JFK), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Montréal (YUL) and New York (JFK) fly about 330 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over New York, and more often in June and October.

- Distance: 330 miles (540 km)
- In the air: about 55 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 4,968
- Severe reports: 28 (0.6%)
- Gusty days: YUL 88, JFK 157

## Questions

### Is YUL to JFK a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YUL–JFK route filed 4,968 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 28 of 4,968 (0.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between YUL and JFK?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over New York, near Albany, about 25 min after leaving YUL, or 25 min after leaving JFK. The next most were over Massachusetts, near Albany, about 35 min after leaving YUL, or 20 min after leaving JFK.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly YUL to JFK?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in September and a bigger share in June and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is JFK to YUL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YUL and JFK?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. YUL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 88 days in the past year, most often in December (13 days). JFK had gusts of 25 knots or more on 157 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
