Dallas-Fort Worth ⇄ Albuquerque

DFW to ABQ turbulence

Flights between Dallas-Fort Worth (DFW) and Albuquerque (ABQ) fly about 570 miles, roughly 1½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over or near Texas, and more often in December and March.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 30 of 6,218 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.5%). Light chop was the level reported most: 2,957 reports.

Questions

Is DFW to ABQ a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DFW–ABQ route filed 6,218 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 30 of 6,218 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. Where is it bumpiest between DFW and ABQ?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over or near Texas, about 30 min after leaving DFW, or 50 min after leaving ABQ. The next most were over New Mexico, about 50 min after leaving DFW, or 30 min after leaving ABQ.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly DFW to ABQ?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in November, July and August and a bigger share in December and March. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is ABQ to DFW the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from ABQ to DFW usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DFW and ABQ?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DFW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 122 days in the past year, most often in March (21 days). ABQ had gusts of 25 knots or more on 152 days in the past year, most often in June (24 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 6,218 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct DFW–ABQ path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
