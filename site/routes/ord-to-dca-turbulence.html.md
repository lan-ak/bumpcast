# ORD to DCA turbulence

> Chicago (ORD) ⇄ Washington (DCA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and Washington (DCA) fly about 610 miles, roughly 1½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Ohio, and more often in April.

- Distance: 610 miles (980 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 30 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 13,462
- Severe reports: 67 (0.5%)
- Gusty days: ORD 123, DCA 99

## Questions

### Is ORD to DCA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–DCA route filed 13,462 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 67 of 13,462 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between ORD and DCA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Ohio, near Columbus, about 40 min after leaving ORD, or 45 min after leaving DCA. The next most were over Indiana, about 20 min after leaving ORD, or 1 h 5 min after leaving DCA.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to DCA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in September and a bigger share in April. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is DCA to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from ORD to DCA usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and DCA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). DCA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 99 days in the past year, most often in March (14 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
