EWR to MCO turbulence

Flights between Newark (EWR) and Orlando (MCO) fly about 940 miles, roughly 2 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Virginia, and more often in November, December and February. Severe reports were rare: 83 of 8,542.

Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Virginia, near Norfolk, about 35 min after leaving EWR (1 h 25 min after leaving MCO): 584 of 876 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms. More on clear-air turbulence and turbulence near clouds.

The next most were near EWR, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving EWR (2 h after leaving MCO): 356 of 732 reports there. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at EWR add to it. More on bumps near the ground and gusty takeoffs and landings.

Takeoff and landing

EWR had gusts of 25 knots or more on 145 days in the past year, most often in December (19 days). MCO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 61 days in the past year, most often in October (9 days). On a gusty day, expect a few minutes of bumps and some sway on the climb out and the way in to land. Why gusty days are bumpy.

Questions

Is EWR to MCO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the EWR–MCO route filed 8,542 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 83 of 8,542 (1.0%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between EWR and MCO?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Virginia, near Norfolk (about 35 min after leaving EWR (1 h 25 min after leaving MCO)), and next most near EWR, where flights climb out or come in to land (about 5 min after leaving EWR (2 h after leaving MCO)).

When is the smoothest time of year to fly EWR to MCO?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in November, December and February. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is MCO to EWR the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at EWR and MCO?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. EWR had gusts of 25 knots or more on 145 days in the past year, most often in December (19 days). MCO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 61 days in the past year, most often in October (9 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 8,542 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct EWR–MCO path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
