# SFO to IAH turbulence

> San Francisco (SFO) ⇄ Houston (IAH), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between San Francisco (SFO) and Houston (IAH) fly about 1,630 miles, roughly 3½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Texas, and more often in February, March and May.

- Distance: 1,630 miles (2,630 km)
- In the air: about 3 h 30 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 9,481
- Severe reports: 27 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: SFO 144, IAH 62

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Texas, about 2 h 45 min after leaving SFO, or 40 min after leaving IAH. There, 379 of 1,479 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

The next most were over Arizona, about 1 h 25 min after leaving SFO, or 1 h 55 min after leaving IAH. There, 249 of 525 reports were moderate or stronger. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height.

## Questions

### Is SFO to IAH a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the SFO–IAH route filed 9,481 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 27 of 9,481 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between SFO and IAH?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Texas, about 2 h 45 min after leaving SFO, or 40 min after leaving IAH. The next most were over Arizona, about 1 h 25 min after leaving SFO, or 1 h 55 min after leaving IAH.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly SFO to IAH?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in February, March and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is IAH to SFO the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from SFO to IAH usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at SFO and IAH?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days). IAH had gusts of 25 knots or more on 62 days in the past year, most often in March (12 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
