Salt Lake City ⇄ Portland

SLC to PDX turbulence

Flights between Salt Lake City (SLC) and Portland (PDX) fly about 630 miles, roughly 1½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near SLC, and more often in November and December.

A year of pilot reports

Strong bumps are rare

Only 8 of 2,115 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.4%). Moderate was the level reported most: 1,117 reports.

Questions

Is SLC to PDX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the SLC–PDX route filed 2,115 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 8 of 2,115 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between SLC and PDX?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near SLC, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving SLC, or 1 h 25 min after leaving PDX. The next most were over Idaho, about 20 min after leaving SLC, or 1 h 5 min after leaving PDX.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly SLC to PDX?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in August and a bigger share in November and December. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is PDX to SLC the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from PDX to SLC usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at SLC and PDX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. SLC had gusts of 25 knots or more on 105 days in the past year, most often in July (14 days). PDX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 40 days in the past year, most often in December (9 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 2,115 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct SLC–PDX path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
