LAX to ORD turbulence

Flights between Los Angeles (LAX) and Chicago (ORD) fly about 1,740 miles, roughly 3¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, and more often in May, June and October. Severe reports were rare: 89 of 26,088.

Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Colorado, near Colorado Springs, about 1 h 45 min after leaving LAX (1 h 55 min after leaving ORD): 863 of 2,423 reports there. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height. More on mountain waves.

Questions

Is LAX to ORD a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAX–ORD route filed 26,088 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 89 of 26,088 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between LAX and ORD?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, near Colorado Springs (about 1 h 45 min after leaving LAX (1 h 55 min after leaving ORD)).

When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAX to ORD?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in April, July and August and a bigger share in May, June and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is ORD to LAX the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAX to ORD usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAX and ORD?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days). ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 26,088 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct LAX–ORD path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
