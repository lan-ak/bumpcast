New York ⇄ Nashville

LGA to BNA turbulence

Flights between New York (LGA) and Nashville (BNA) fly about 760 miles, roughly 1¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over West Virginia, and more often in December and February.

Questions

Is LGA to BNA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LGA–BNA route filed 11,852 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 59 of 11,852 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between LGA and BNA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over West Virginia, about 1 h after leaving LGA, or 45 min after leaving BNA. The next most were over Kentucky, near Knoxville/Maryville, about 1 h 15 min after leaving LGA, or 25 min after leaving BNA.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly LGA to BNA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in August and September and a bigger share in December and February. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is BNA to LGA the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from BNA to LGA usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LGA and BNA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LGA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 146 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days). BNA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 97 days in the past year, most often in March (17 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 11,852 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct LGA–BNA path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
