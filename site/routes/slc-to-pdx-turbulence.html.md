# SLC to PDX turbulence

> Salt Lake City (SLC) ⇄ Portland (PDX), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Salt Lake City (SLC) and Portland (PDX) fly about 630 miles, roughly 1½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near SLC, and more often in November and December.

- Distance: 630 miles (1,010 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 30 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 2,115
- Severe reports: 8 (0.4%)
- Gusty days: SLC 105, PDX 40

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often near SLC, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving SLC, or 1 h 25 min after leaving PDX. There, 153 of 518 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at SLC add to it.

The next most were over Idaho, about 20 min after leaving SLC, or 1 h 5 min after leaving PDX. There, 118 of 170 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is SLC to PDX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the SLC–PDX route filed 2,115 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 8 of 2,115 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between SLC and PDX?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near SLC, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving SLC, or 1 h 25 min after leaving PDX. The next most were over Idaho, about 20 min after leaving SLC, or 1 h 5 min after leaving PDX.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly SLC to PDX?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in August and a bigger share in November and December. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is PDX to SLC the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from PDX to SLC usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at SLC and PDX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. SLC had gusts of 25 knots or more on 105 days in the past year, most often in July (14 days). PDX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 40 days in the past year, most often in December (9 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
