# ATL to AUS turbulence

> Atlanta (ATL) ⇄ Austin (AUS), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Atlanta (ATL) and Austin (AUS) fly about 810 miles, roughly 2 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Alabama, and more often in December, April and May.

- Distance: 810 miles (1,310 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 55 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 11,627
- Severe reports: 33 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: ATL 65, AUS 91

## Questions

### Is ATL to AUS a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ATL–AUS route filed 11,627 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 33 of 11,627 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between ATL and AUS?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Alabama, near Birmingham, about 20 min after leaving ATL, or 1 h 25 min after leaving AUS. The next most were over Louisiana, about 1 h 5 min after leaving ATL, or 40 min after leaving AUS.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ATL to AUS?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, September and October and a bigger share in December, April and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is AUS to ATL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from AUS to ATL usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ATL and AUS?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ATL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 65 days in the past year, most often in March (10 days). AUS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 91 days in the past year, most often in March (18 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
