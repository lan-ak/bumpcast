Calgary ⇄ Las Vegas

YYC to LAS turbulence

Flights between Calgary (YYC) and Las Vegas (LAS) fly about 1,040 miles, roughly 2¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over or near Nevada, and more often in November and December.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 20 of 2,539 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.8%). Moderate was the level reported most: 1,402 reports.

Questions

Is YYC to LAS a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YYC–LAS route filed 2,539 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 20 of 2,539 (0.8%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between YYC and LAS?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over or near Nevada, about 1 h 50 min after leaving YYC, or 25 min after leaving LAS. The next most were over Nevada, about 1 h 30 min after leaving YYC, or 45 min after leaving LAS.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly YYC to LAS?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in April, June and August and a bigger share in November and December. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is LAS to YYC the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YYC and LAS?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. YYC had gusts of 25 knots or more on 121 days in the past year, most often in June (17 days). LAS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 109 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 2,539 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct YYC–LAS path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
