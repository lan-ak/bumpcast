Dallas-Fort Worth ⇄ New Orleans

DFW to MSY turbulence

Flights between Dallas-Fort Worth (DFW) and New Orleans (MSY) fly about 450 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Louisiana, and more often in December, April and May.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 26 of 5,287 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.5%). Light chop was the level reported most: 2,426 reports.

Questions

Is DFW to MSY a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DFW–MSY route filed 5,287 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 26 of 5,287 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between DFW and MSY?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Louisiana, about 40 min after leaving DFW, or 25 min after leaving MSY. The next most were over Texas, about 30 min after leaving DFW, or 35 min after leaving MSY.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly DFW to MSY?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in August, September and October and a bigger share in December, April and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is MSY to DFW the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DFW and MSY?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DFW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 122 days in the past year, most often in March (21 days). MSY had gusts of 25 knots or more on 62 days in the past year, most often in January (11 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 5,287 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct DFW–MSY path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
