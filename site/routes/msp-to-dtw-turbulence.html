Minneapolis ⇄ Detroit

MSP to DTW turbulence

Flights between Minneapolis (MSP) and Detroit (DTW) fly about 530 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Wisconsin, and more often in April, May and June.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 20 of 8,117 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.2%). Light chop was the level reported most: 4,305 reports.

Questions

Is MSP to DTW a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the MSP–DTW route filed 8,117 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 20 of 8,117 (0.2%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between MSP and DTW?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Wisconsin, near Milwaukee, about 40 min after leaving MSP, or 35 min after leaving DTW. The next most were over Michigan, near Grand Rapids, about 55 min after leaving MSP, or 20 min after leaving DTW.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly MSP to DTW?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in January, February and October and a bigger share in April, May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is DTW to MSP the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from MSP to DTW usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at MSP and DTW?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. MSP had gusts of 25 knots or more on 127 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). DTW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 106 days in the past year, most often in January (14 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 8,117 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct MSP–DTW path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
