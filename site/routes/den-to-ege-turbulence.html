Denver ⇄ Eagle

DEN to EGE turbulence

Flights between Denver (DEN) and Eagle (EGE) fly about 120 miles, roughly 30 minutes in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, and more often in December, January and June.

A year of pilot reports

Strong bumps are rare

Only 92 of 8,921 pilot reports near this route were severe (1.0%). Light chop was the level reported most: 3,259 reports.

Takeoff and landing

DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). EGE had gusts of 25 knots or more on 122 days in the past year, most often in June (24 days). Questions

Is DEN to EGE a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DEN–EGE route filed 8,921 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 92 of 8,921 (1.0%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between DEN and EGE?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, near Denver, about 10 min after leaving DEN, or 20 min after leaving EGE. The next most were near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 0 min after leaving DEN, or 30 min after leaving EGE.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly DEN to EGE?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in August, September and October and a bigger share in December, January and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is EGE to DEN the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DEN and EGE?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). EGE had gusts of 25 knots or more on 122 days in the past year, most often in June (24 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 8,921 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct DEN–EGE path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
