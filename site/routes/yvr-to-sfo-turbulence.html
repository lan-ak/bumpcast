Vancouver ⇄ San Francisco

YVR to SFO turbulence

Flights between Vancouver (YVR) and San Francisco (SFO) fly about 800 miles, roughly 1¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Oregon, and more often in November.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 6 of 1,891 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.3%). Moderate was the level reported most: 1,022 reports.

Takeoff and landing

Questions

Is YVR to SFO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YVR–SFO route filed 1,891 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 6 of 1,891 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between YVR and SFO?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Oregon, near Portland, about 30 min after leaving YVR, or 1 h 15 min after leaving SFO. The next most were over California, about 1 h 5 min after leaving YVR, or 40 min after leaving SFO.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly YVR to SFO?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in March and July and a bigger share in November. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is SFO to YVR the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YVR and SFO?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. YVR had gusts of 25 knots or more on 32 days in the past year, most often in December (8 days). SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 1,891 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct YVR–SFO path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
