# BOS to MIA turbulence

> Boston (BOS) ⇄ Miami (MIA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Boston (BOS) and Miami (MIA) fly about 1,260 miles, roughly 2¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over New York, and more often in November, December and February.

- Distance: 1,260 miles (2,030 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 45 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 9,575
- Severe reports: 43 (0.4%)
- Gusty days: BOS 148, MIA 58

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over New York, near Providence/Warwick, about 15 min after leaving BOS, or 2 h 25 min after leaving MIA. There, 348 of 958 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is BOS to MIA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the BOS–MIA route filed 9,575 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 43 of 9,575 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between BOS and MIA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over New York, near Providence/Warwick, about 15 min after leaving BOS, or 2 h 25 min after leaving MIA.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly BOS to MIA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in November, December and February. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is MIA to BOS the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from MIA to BOS usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at BOS and MIA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. BOS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 148 days in the past year, most often in December (23 days). MIA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 58 days in the past year, most often in April (9 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
