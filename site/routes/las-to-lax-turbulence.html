LAS to LAX turbulence

Flights between Las Vegas (LAS) and Los Angeles (LAX) fly about 240 miles, roughly 45 minutes in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Nevada, and more often in January, February and March. Severe reports were rare: 18 of 1,071.

Questions

Is LAS to LAX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAS–LAX route filed 1,071 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 18 of 1,071 (1.7%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between LAS and LAX?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Nevada, near Las Vegas (about 5 min after leaving LAS (40 min after leaving LAX)), and next most over California, near Ontario (about 35 min after leaving LAS (10 min after leaving LAX)).

When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAS to LAX?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in November, July and August and a bigger share in January, February and March. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is LAX to LAS the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAS and LAX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 109 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 1,071 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct LAS–LAX path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
