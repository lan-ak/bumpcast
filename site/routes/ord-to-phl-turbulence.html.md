# ORD to PHL turbulence

> Chicago (ORD) ⇄ Philadelphia (PHL), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and Philadelphia (PHL) fly about 680 miles, roughly 1½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Ohio, and more often in April.

- Distance: 680 miles (1,090 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 35 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 11,034
- Severe reports: 68 (0.6%)
- Gusty days: ORD 123, PHL 118

## Questions

### Is ORD to PHL a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–PHL route filed 11,034 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 68 of 11,034 (0.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between ORD and PHL?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Ohio, near Detroit, about 30 min after leaving ORD, or 1 h after leaving PHL. The next most were over Pennsylvania, near Pittsburgh, about 55 min after leaving ORD, or 35 min after leaving PHL.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to PHL?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in September and a bigger share in April. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is PHL to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from ORD to PHL usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and PHL?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). PHL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 118 days in the past year, most often in May (17 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
