Newark ⇄ Las Vegas

EWR to LAS turbulence

Flights between Newark (EWR) and Las Vegas (LAS) fly about 2,220 miles, roughly 4½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Ohio, and more often in May.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 104 of 34,218 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.3%). Light chop was the level reported most: 16,624 reports.

Questions

Is EWR to LAS a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the EWR–LAS route filed 34,218 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 104 of 34,218 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between EWR and LAS?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Ohio, near Cleveland, about 50 min after leaving EWR, or 3 h 40 min after leaving LAS. The next most were over Illinois, near Chicago, about 1 h 30 min after leaving EWR, or 3 h after leaving LAS.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly EWR to LAS?

Moderate bumps made up a bigger share of reports in May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is LAS to EWR the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAS to EWR usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at EWR and LAS?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. EWR had gusts of 25 knots or more on 145 days in the past year, most often in December (19 days). LAS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 109 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 34,218 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct EWR–LAS path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
