Sacramento ⇄ San Diego

SMF to SAN turbulence

Flights between Sacramento (SMF) and San Diego (SAN) fly about 480 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over California, and more often in March.

A year of pilot reports

Strong bumps are rare

Only 13 of 1,071 pilot reports near this route were severe (1.2%). Moderate was the level reported most: 578 reports.

Takeoff and landing

SMF had gusts of 25 knots or more on 33 days in the past year, most often in May (7 days). SAN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 20 days in the past year, most often in February (4 days). Questions

Is SMF to SAN a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the SMF–SAN route filed 1,071 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 13 of 1,071 (1.2%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between SMF and SAN?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over California, near Santa Ana, about 55 min after leaving SMF, or 15 min after leaving SAN.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly SMF to SAN?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in June and July and a bigger share in March. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is SAN to SMF the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at SMF and SAN?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. SMF had gusts of 25 knots or more on 33 days in the past year, most often in May (7 days). SAN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 20 days in the past year, most often in February (4 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 1,071 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct SMF–SAN path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
