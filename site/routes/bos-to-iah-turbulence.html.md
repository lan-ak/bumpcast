# BOS to IAH turbulence

> Boston (BOS) ⇄ Houston (IAH), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Boston (BOS) and Houston (IAH) fly about 1,590 miles, roughly 3½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Louisiana, and more often in December and May.

- Distance: 1,590 miles (2,570 km)
- In the air: about 3 h 25 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 29,098
- Severe reports: 98 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: BOS 148, IAH 62

## Questions

### Is BOS to IAH a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the BOS–IAH route filed 29,098 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 98 of 29,098 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between BOS and IAH?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Louisiana, about 2 h 50 min after leaving BOS, or 30 min after leaving IAH. The next most were over Tennessee, near Knoxville/Maryville, about 1 h 45 min after leaving BOS, or 1 h 35 min after leaving IAH.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly BOS to IAH?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, September and October and a bigger share in December and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is IAH to BOS the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from IAH to BOS usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at BOS and IAH?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. BOS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 148 days in the past year, most often in December (23 days). IAH had gusts of 25 knots or more on 62 days in the past year, most often in March (12 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
