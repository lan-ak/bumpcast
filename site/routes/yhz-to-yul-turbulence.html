Halifax ⇄ Montréal

YHZ to YUL turbulence

Flights between Halifax (YHZ) and Montréal (YUL) fly about 500 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Maine, and more often in December, May and October.

A year of pilot reports

Strong bumps are rare

Only 6 of 1,197 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.5%). Light chop was the level reported most: 556 reports.

Questions

Is YHZ to YUL a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YHZ–YUL route filed 1,197 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 6 of 1,197 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between YHZ and YUL?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Maine, about 35 min after leaving YHZ, or 35 min after leaving YUL.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly YHZ to YUL?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in November, January and June and a bigger share in December, May and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is YUL to YHZ the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from YUL to YHZ usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YHZ and YUL?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. YHZ had gusts of 25 knots or more on 135 days in the past year, most often in December (19 days). YUL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 88 days in the past year, most often in December (13 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 1,197 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct YHZ–YUL path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
