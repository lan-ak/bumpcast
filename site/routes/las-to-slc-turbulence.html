Las Vegas ⇄ Salt Lake City

LAS to SLC turbulence

Flights between Las Vegas (LAS) and Salt Lake City (SLC) fly about 370 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Utah, and more often in December, May and June.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 4 of 1,200 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.3%). Moderate was the level reported most: 556 reports.

Questions

Is LAS to SLC a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAS–SLC route filed 1,200 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 4 of 1,200 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between LAS and SLC?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Utah, near Salt Lake City, about 50 min after leaving LAS, or 5 min after leaving SLC. The next most were over or near Utah, about 25 min after leaving LAS, or 30 min after leaving SLC.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAS to SLC?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in November, July and August and a bigger share in December, May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is SLC to LAS the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAS and SLC?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 109 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). SLC had gusts of 25 knots or more on 105 days in the past year, most often in July (14 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 1,200 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct LAS–SLC path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
