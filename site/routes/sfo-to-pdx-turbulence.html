San Francisco ⇄ Portland

SFO to PDX turbulence

Flights between San Francisco (SFO) and Portland (PDX) fly about 550 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Oregon, and more often in November.

A year of pilot reports

Strong bumps are rare

Only 6 of 1,053 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.6%). Moderate was the level reported most: 591 reports.

Takeoff and landing

SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days). PDX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 40 days in the past year, most often in December (9 days). Questions

Is SFO to PDX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the SFO–PDX route filed 1,053 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 6 of 1,053 (0.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between SFO and PDX?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Oregon, about 45 min after leaving SFO, or 35 min after leaving PDX. The next most were near SFO, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving SFO, or 1 h 20 min after leaving PDX.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly SFO to PDX?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in March, August and September and a bigger share in November. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is PDX to SFO the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at SFO and PDX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days). PDX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 40 days in the past year, most often in December (9 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 1,053 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct SFO–PDX path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
