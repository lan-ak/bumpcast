# JFK to LAX turbulence

> New York (JFK) ⇄ Los Angeles (LAX), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between New York (JFK) and Los Angeles (LAX) fly about 2,470 miles, roughly 5 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado. Severe reports were rare: 120 of 35,528.

- Distance: 2,470 miles (3,970 km)
- In the air: about 5 h 5 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 35,528
- Severe reports: 120 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: JFK 157, LAX 17

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Colorado, near Colorado Springs, about 3 h 20 min after leaving JFK (1 h 40 min after leaving LAX): 1,072 of 2,964 reports there. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height.

The next most were over Pennsylvania, near Pittsburgh, about 40 min after leaving JFK (4 h 20 min after leaving LAX): 1,056 of 1,571 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is JFK to LAX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the JFK–LAX route filed 35,528 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 120 of 35,528 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Is LAX to JFK the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAX to JFK usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
