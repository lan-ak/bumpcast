# DFW to ORD turbulence

> Dallas-Fort Worth (DFW) ⇄ Chicago (ORD), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Dallas-Fort Worth (DFW) and Chicago (ORD) fly about 800 miles, roughly 1¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, and more often in March, April and May. Severe reports were rare: 54 of 11,184.

- Distance: 800 miles (1,290 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 50 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 11,184
- Severe reports: 54 (0.5%)
- Gusty days: DFW 122, ORD 123

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 1 h 45 min after leaving DFW (5 min after leaving ORD): 291 of 2,116 reports there. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at ORD add to it.

The next most were over Arkansas, about 40 min after leaving DFW (1 h 5 min after leaving ORD): 252 of 621 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is DFW to ORD a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DFW–ORD route filed 11,184 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 54 of 11,184 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between DFW and ORD?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land (about 1 h 45 min after leaving DFW (5 min after leaving ORD)), and next most over Arkansas (about 40 min after leaving DFW (1 h 5 min after leaving ORD)).

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly DFW to ORD?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in March, April and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is ORD to DFW the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from DFW to ORD usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DFW and ORD?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DFW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 122 days in the past year, most often in March (21 days). ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
