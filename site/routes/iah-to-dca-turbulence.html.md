# IAH to DCA turbulence

> Houston (IAH) ⇄ Washington (DCA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Houston (IAH) and Washington (DCA) fly about 1,210 miles, roughly 2¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Louisiana, and more often in April and May.

- Distance: 1,210 miles (1,940 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 40 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 22,379
- Severe reports: 68 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: IAH 62, DCA 99

## Questions

### Is IAH to DCA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the IAH–DCA route filed 22,379 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 68 of 22,379 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between IAH and DCA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Louisiana, about 30 min after leaving IAH, or 2 h 5 min after leaving DCA. The next most were over Tennessee, about 1 h 50 min after leaving IAH, or 45 min after leaving DCA.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly IAH to DCA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, September and October and a bigger share in April and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is DCA to IAH the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from IAH to DCA usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at IAH and DCA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. IAH had gusts of 25 knots or more on 62 days in the past year, most often in March (12 days). DCA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 99 days in the past year, most often in March (14 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
