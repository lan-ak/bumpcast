DEN to DFW turbulence

Flights between Denver (DEN) and Dallas-Fort Worth (DFW) fly about 640 miles, roughly 1½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, and more often in December. Severe reports were rare: 57 of 13,137.

Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving DEN (1 h 25 min after leaving DFW): 869 of 3,733 reports there. Close to the ground, wind over the mountains stirs the air, and gusty days at DEN add to it. More on bumps near the ground, mountain waves and gusty takeoffs and landings.

Takeoff and landing

DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). DFW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 122 days in the past year, most often in March (21 days). Questions

Is DEN to DFW a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DEN–DFW route filed 13,137 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 57 of 13,137 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between DEN and DFW?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land (about 5 min after leaving DEN (1 h 25 min after leaving DFW)).

When is the smoothest time of year to fly DEN to DFW?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in August and September and a bigger share in December. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is DFW to DEN the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DEN and DFW?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). DFW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 122 days in the past year, most often in March (21 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 13,137 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct DEN–DFW path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
