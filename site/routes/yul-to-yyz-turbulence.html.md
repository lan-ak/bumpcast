# YUL to YYZ turbulence

> Montréal (YUL) ⇄ Toronto (YYZ), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Montréal (YUL) and Toronto (YYZ) fly about 320 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Ontario, and more often in December, May and October. Severe reports were rare: 9 of 447.

- Distance: 320 miles (510 km)
- In the air: about 55 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 447
- Severe reports: 9 (2.0%)
- Gusty days: YUL 88, YYZ 120

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Ontario, near Ottawa, about 20 min after leaving YUL (30 min after leaving YYZ): 34 of 128 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is YUL to YYZ a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YUL–YYZ route filed 447 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 9 of 447 (2.0%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. Moderate bumps were reported most often over Ontario, near Ottawa, about 20 min after leaving YUL (30 min after leaving YYZ): 34 of 128 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly YUL to YYZ?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in March, April and September and a bigger share in December, May and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is YYZ to YUL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YUL and YYZ?

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
