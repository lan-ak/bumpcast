# SLC to BOI turbulence

> Salt Lake City (SLC) ⇄ Boise (BOI), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Salt Lake City (SLC) and Boise (BOI) fly about 290 miles, roughly 0¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near SLC, and more often in December and June.

- Distance: 290 miles (470 km)
- In the air: about 50 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 1,108
- Severe reports: 7 (0.6%)
- Gusty days: SLC 105, BOI 60

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often near SLC, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving SLC, or 50 min after leaving BOI. There, 81 of 282 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at SLC add to it.

## Questions

### Is SLC to BOI a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the SLC–BOI route filed 1,108 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 7 of 1,108 (0.6%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between SLC and BOI?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near SLC, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving SLC, or 50 min after leaving BOI.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly SLC to BOI?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in January, July and August and a bigger share in December and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is BOI to SLC the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at SLC and BOI?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. SLC had gusts of 25 knots or more on 105 days in the past year, most often in July (14 days). BOI had gusts of 25 knots or more on 60 days in the past year, most often in May (13 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
