# ORD to PDX turbulence

> Chicago (ORD) ⇄ Portland (PDX), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and Portland (PDX) fly about 1,730 miles, roughly 3¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, and more often in June.

- Distance: 1,730 miles (2,790 km)
- In the air: about 3 h 40 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 15,113
- Severe reports: 58 (0.4%)
- Gusty days: ORD 123, PDX 40

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 10 min after leaving ORD, or 3 h 30 min after leaving PDX. There, 569 of 2,793 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at ORD add to it.

The next most were over Iowa, about 40 min after leaving ORD, or 2 h 55 min after leaving PDX. There, 408 of 1,947 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is ORD to PDX a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–PDX route filed 15,113 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 58 of 15,113 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between ORD and PDX?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 10 min after leaving ORD, or 3 h 30 min after leaving PDX. The next most were over Iowa, about 40 min after leaving ORD, or 2 h 55 min after leaving PDX.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to PDX?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in January and July and a bigger share in June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is PDX to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from PDX to ORD usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and PDX?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). PDX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 40 days in the past year, most often in December (9 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
