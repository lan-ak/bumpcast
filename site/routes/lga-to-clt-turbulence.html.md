# LGA to CLT turbulence

> New York (LGA) ⇄ Charlotte (CLT), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between New York (LGA) and Charlotte (CLT) fly about 540 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over New Jersey.

- Distance: 540 miles (870 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 20 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 4,981
- Severe reports: 54 (1.1%)
- Gusty days: LGA 146, CLT 49

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over New Jersey, near Newark, about 10 min after leaving LGA, or 1 h 10 min after leaving CLT. There, 200 of 482 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at LGA add to it.

The next most were over Virginia, near Richmond, about 45 min after leaving LGA, or 30 min after leaving CLT. There, 179 of 278 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is LGA to CLT a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LGA–CLT route filed 4,981 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 54 of 4,981 (1.1%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between LGA and CLT?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over New Jersey, near Newark, about 10 min after leaving LGA, or 1 h 10 min after leaving CLT. The next most were over Virginia, near Richmond, about 45 min after leaving LGA, or 30 min after leaving CLT.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly LGA to CLT?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in January, March and April. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is CLT to LGA the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LGA and CLT?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LGA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 146 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days). CLT had gusts of 25 knots or more on 49 days in the past year, most often in February (8 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
