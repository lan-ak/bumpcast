# CLT to MCO turbulence

> Charlotte (CLT) ⇄ Orlando (MCO), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Charlotte (CLT) and Orlando (MCO) fly about 470 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Georgia, and more often in December, February and March.

- Distance: 470 miles (760 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 15 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 6,503
- Severe reports: 21 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: CLT 49, MCO 61

## Questions

### Is CLT to MCO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the CLT–MCO route filed 6,503 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 21 of 6,503 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between CLT and MCO?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Georgia, near Savannah, about 35 min after leaving CLT, or 35 min after leaving MCO. The next most were over South Carolina, near Savannah, about 25 min after leaving CLT, or 45 min after leaving MCO.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly CLT to MCO?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in December, February and March. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is MCO to CLT the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at CLT and MCO?

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
