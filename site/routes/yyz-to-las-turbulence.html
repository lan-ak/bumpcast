Toronto ⇄ Las Vegas

YYZ to LAS turbulence

Flights between Toronto (YYZ) and Las Vegas (LAS) fly about 1,940 miles, roughly 4 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Iowa, and more often in May and June.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 102 of 31,021 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.3%). Light chop was the level reported most: 15,454 reports.

Questions

Is YYZ to LAS a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YYZ–LAS route filed 31,021 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 102 of 31,021 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between YYZ and LAS?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Iowa, near Des Moines, about 1 h 30 min after leaving YYZ, or 2 h 30 min after leaving LAS. The next most were over Colorado, near Denver, about 2 h 40 min after leaving YYZ, or 1 h 20 min after leaving LAS.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly YYZ to LAS?

Moderate bumps made up a bigger share of reports in May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is LAS to YYZ the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAS to YYZ usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YYZ and LAS?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. YYZ had gusts of 25 knots or more on 120 days in the past year, most often in January (18 days). LAS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 109 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 31,021 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct YYZ–LAS path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
