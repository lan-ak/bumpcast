Detroit ⇄ Denver

DTW to DEN turbulence

Flights between Detroit (DTW) and Denver (DEN) fly about 1,120 miles, roughly 2½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, and more often in May and June.

A year of pilot reports

Strong bumps are rare

Only 69 of 24,009 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.3%). Light chop was the level reported most: 11,660 reports.

Takeoff and landing

DTW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 106 days in the past year, most often in January (14 days). DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). On a gusty day, expect a few minutes of bumps and some sway on the climb out and the way in to land. Why gusty days are bumpy.

Questions

Is DTW to DEN a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DTW–DEN route filed 24,009 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 69 of 24,009 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between DTW and DEN?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 2 h 20 min after leaving DTW, or 5 min after leaving DEN.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly DTW to DEN?

Moderate bumps made up a bigger share of reports in May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is DEN to DTW the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from DEN to DTW usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DTW and DEN?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DTW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 106 days in the past year, most often in January (14 days). DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 24,009 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct DTW–DEN path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
