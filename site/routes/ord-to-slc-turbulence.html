Chicago ⇄ Salt Lake City

ORD to SLC turbulence

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and Salt Lake City (SLC) fly about 1,250 miles, roughly 2¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, and more often in June.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 77 of 20,329 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.4%). Light chop was the level reported most: 9,612 reports.

Questions

Is ORD to SLC a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–SLC route filed 20,329 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 77 of 20,329 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between ORD and SLC?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving ORD, or 2 h 35 min after leaving SLC. The next most were over Iowa, near Omaha, about 55 min after leaving ORD, or 1 h 45 min after leaving SLC.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to SLC?

Moderate bumps made up a bigger share of reports in June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is SLC to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from SLC to ORD usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and SLC?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). SLC had gusts of 25 knots or more on 105 days in the past year, most often in July (14 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 20,329 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct ORD–SLC path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
