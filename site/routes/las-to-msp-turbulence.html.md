# LAS to MSP turbulence

> Las Vegas (LAS) ⇄ Minneapolis (MSP), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Las Vegas (LAS) and Minneapolis (MSP) fly about 1,300 miles, roughly 2¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, and more often in June, September and October.

- Distance: 1,300 miles (2,090 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 50 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 15,055
- Severe reports: 68 (0.5%)
- Gusty days: LAS 109, MSP 127

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Colorado, about 1 h 10 min after leaving LAS, or 1 h 35 min after leaving MSP. There, 340 of 1,642 reports were moderate or stronger. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height.

The next most were over Nebraska, about 1 h 35 min after leaving LAS, or 1 h 10 min after leaving MSP. There, 300 of 1,265 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is LAS to MSP a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAS–MSP route filed 15,055 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 68 of 15,055 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between LAS and MSP?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, about 1 h 10 min after leaving LAS, or 1 h 35 min after leaving MSP. The next most were over Nebraska, about 1 h 35 min after leaving LAS, or 1 h 10 min after leaving MSP.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAS to MSP?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in January, February and July and a bigger share in June, September and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is MSP to LAS the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAS to MSP usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAS and MSP?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 109 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). MSP had gusts of 25 knots or more on 127 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
