# DFW to MSY turbulence

> Dallas-Fort Worth (DFW) ⇄ New Orleans (MSY), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Dallas-Fort Worth (DFW) and New Orleans (MSY) fly about 450 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Louisiana, and more often in December, April and May.

- Distance: 450 miles (720 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 10 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 5,287
- Severe reports: 26 (0.5%)
- Gusty days: DFW 122, MSY 62

## Questions

### Is DFW to MSY a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DFW–MSY route filed 5,287 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 26 of 5,287 (0.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### Where is it bumpiest between DFW and MSY?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Louisiana, about 40 min after leaving DFW, or 25 min after leaving MSY. The next most were over Texas, about 30 min after leaving DFW, or 35 min after leaving MSY.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly DFW to MSY?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in August, September and October and a bigger share in December, April and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is MSY to DFW the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DFW and MSY?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DFW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 122 days in the past year, most often in March (21 days). MSY had gusts of 25 knots or more on 62 days in the past year, most often in January (11 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
