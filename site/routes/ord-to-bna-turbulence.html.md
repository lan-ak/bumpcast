# ORD to BNA turbulence

> Chicago (ORD) ⇄ Nashville (BNA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and Nashville (BNA) fly about 410 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Illinois, and more often in April.

- Distance: 410 miles (660 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 5 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 11,032
- Severe reports: 42 (0.4%)
- Gusty days: ORD 123, BNA 97

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Illinois, near Chicago, about 5 min after leaving ORD, or 55 min after leaving BNA. There, 255 of 932 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at ORD add to it.

The next most were over Indiana, near Indianapolis, about 25 min after leaving ORD, or 35 min after leaving BNA. There, 236 of 1,001 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is ORD to BNA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–BNA route filed 11,032 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 42 of 11,032 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between ORD and BNA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Illinois, near Chicago, about 5 min after leaving ORD, or 55 min after leaving BNA. The next most were over Indiana, near Indianapolis, about 25 min after leaving ORD, or 35 min after leaving BNA.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to BNA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July and September and a bigger share in April. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is BNA to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and BNA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). BNA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 97 days in the past year, most often in March (17 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
