# DEN to DCA turbulence

> Denver (DEN) ⇄ Washington (DCA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Denver (DEN) and Washington (DCA) fly about 1,470 miles, roughly 3¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near DEN.

- Distance: 1,470 miles (2,370 km)
- In the air: about 3 h 10 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 32,536
- Severe reports: 112 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: DEN 155, DCA 99

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 10 min after leaving DEN, or 3 h after leaving DCA. There, 1,129 of 4,569 reports were moderate or stronger. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height.

The next most were over Indiana, near Indianapolis, about 2 h 5 min after leaving DEN, or 1 h after leaving DCA. There, 742 of 3,460 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is DEN to DCA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DEN–DCA route filed 32,536 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 112 of 32,536 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between DEN and DCA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 10 min after leaving DEN, or 3 h after leaving DCA. The next most were over Indiana, near Indianapolis, about 2 h 5 min after leaving DEN, or 1 h after leaving DCA.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly DEN to DCA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in August and September. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is DCA to DEN the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from DEN to DCA usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DEN and DCA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). DCA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 99 days in the past year, most often in March (14 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
