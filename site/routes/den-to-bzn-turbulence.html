Denver ⇄ Bozeman

DEN to BZN turbulence

Flights between Denver (DEN) and Bozeman (BZN) fly about 520 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, and more often in December and June.

A year of pilot reports

Strong bumps are rare

Only 70 of 9,139 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.8%). Light chop was the level reported most: 3,474 reports.

Takeoff and landing

DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). BZN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 108 days in the past year, most often in March (17 days). On a gusty day, expect a few minutes of bumps and some sway on the climb out and the way in to land. Why gusty days are bumpy.

Questions

Is DEN to BZN a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DEN–BZN route filed 9,139 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 70 of 9,139 (0.8%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between DEN and BZN?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving DEN, or 1 h 15 min after leaving BZN.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly DEN to BZN?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and October and a bigger share in December and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is BZN to DEN the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DEN and BZN?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). BZN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 108 days in the past year, most often in March (17 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 9,139 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct DEN–BZN path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
