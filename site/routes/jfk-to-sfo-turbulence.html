JFK to SFO turbulence

Flights between New York (JFK) and San Francisco (SFO) fly about 2,580 miles, roughly 5¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near Cleveland. Severe reports were rare: 114 of 32,267.

Takeoff and landing

JFK had gusts of 25 knots or more on 157 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days). SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days). Questions

Is JFK to SFO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the JFK–SFO route filed 32,267 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 114 of 32,267 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between JFK and SFO?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near Cleveland (about 1 h after leaving JFK (4 h 15 min after leaving SFO)), and next most near JFK, where flights climb out or come in to land (about 10 min after leaving JFK (5 h 5 min after leaving SFO)).

When is the smoothest time of year to fly JFK to SFO?

Moderate bumps made up a similar share of reports all year. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is SFO to JFK the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from SFO to JFK usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at JFK and SFO?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. JFK had gusts of 25 knots or more on 157 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days). SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 32,267 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct JFK–SFO path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
