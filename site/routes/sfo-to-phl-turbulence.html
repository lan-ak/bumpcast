San Francisco ⇄ Philadelphia

SFO to PHL turbulence

Flights between San Francisco (SFO) and Philadelphia (PHL) fly about 2,520 miles, roughly 5¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Ohio.

Strong bumps are rare

Questions

Is SFO to PHL a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the SFO–PHL route filed 33,604 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 121 of 33,604 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between SFO and PHL?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Ohio, near Pittsburgh, about 4 h 25 min after leaving SFO, or 40 min after leaving PHL. The next most were over Nebraska, about 2 h 40 min after leaving SFO, or 2 h 25 min after leaving PHL.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly SFO to PHL?

Moderate bumps made up a similar share of reports all year. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is PHL to SFO the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from SFO to PHL usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at SFO and PHL?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days). PHL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 118 days in the past year, most often in May (17 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 33,604 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct SFO–PHL path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
