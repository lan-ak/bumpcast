# ATL to SFO turbulence

> Atlanta (ATL) ⇄ San Francisco (SFO), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Atlanta (ATL) and San Francisco (SFO) fly about 2,130 miles, roughly 4½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, and more often in May.

- Distance: 2,130 miles (3,440 km)
- In the air: about 4 h 25 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 25,016
- Severe reports: 70 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: ATL 65, SFO 144

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Colorado, about 2 h 15 min after leaving ATL, or 2 h 5 min after leaving SFO. There, 987 of 2,248 reports were moderate or stronger. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height.

The next most were over Arkansas, near Memphis, about 50 min after leaving ATL, or 3 h 30 min after leaving SFO. There, 842 of 2,005 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is ATL to SFO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ATL–SFO route filed 25,016 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 70 of 25,016 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between ATL and SFO?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, about 2 h 15 min after leaving ATL, or 2 h 5 min after leaving SFO. The next most were over Arkansas, near Memphis, about 50 min after leaving ATL, or 3 h 30 min after leaving SFO.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ATL to SFO?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July and a bigger share in May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is SFO to ATL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from SFO to ATL usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ATL and SFO?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ATL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 65 days in the past year, most often in March (10 days). SFO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 144 days in the past year, most often in July (24 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
