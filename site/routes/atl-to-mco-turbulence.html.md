# ATL to MCO turbulence

> Atlanta (ATL) ⇄ Orlando (MCO), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Atlanta (ATL) and Orlando (MCO) fly about 400 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Georgia, and more often in December, February and April. Severe reports were rare: 21 of 7,070.

- Distance: 400 miles (650 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 5 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 7,070
- Severe reports: 21 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: ATL 65, MCO 61

## Questions

### Is ATL to MCO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ATL–MCO route filed 7,070 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 21 of 7,070 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ATL to MCO?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in December, February and April. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is MCO to ATL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
