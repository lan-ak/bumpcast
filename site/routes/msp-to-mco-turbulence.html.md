# MSP to MCO turbulence

> Minneapolis (MSP) ⇄ Orlando (MCO), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Minneapolis (MSP) and Orlando (MCO) fly about 1,310 miles, roughly 2¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Tennessee, and more often in March, April and May.

- Distance: 1,310 miles (2,110 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 50 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 30,400
- Severe reports: 76 (0.2%)
- Gusty days: MSP 127, MCO 61

## Questions

### Is MSP to MCO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the MSP–MCO route filed 30,400 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 76 of 30,400 (0.2%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between MSP and MCO?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Tennessee, near Nashville, about 1 h 25 min after leaving MSP, or 1 h 20 min after leaving MCO. The next most were over Georgia, near Atlanta, about 1 h 50 min after leaving MSP, or 55 min after leaving MCO.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly MSP to MCO?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in March, April and May. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is MCO to MSP the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from MSP to MCO usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at MSP and MCO?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. MSP had gusts of 25 knots or more on 127 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). MCO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 61 days in the past year, most often in October (9 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
