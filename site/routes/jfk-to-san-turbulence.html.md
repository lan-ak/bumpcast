# JFK to SAN turbulence

> New York (JFK) ⇄ San Diego (SAN), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between New York (JFK) and San Diego (SAN) fly about 2,440 miles, roughly 5 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado.

- Distance: 2,440 miles (3,930 km)
- In the air: about 5 h (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 32,307
- Severe reports: 135 (0.4%)
- Gusty days: JFK 157, SAN 20

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Colorado, about 3 h 20 min after leaving JFK, or 1 h 40 min after leaving SAN. There, 1,009 of 2,249 reports were moderate or stronger. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height.

The next most were over Indiana, near Indianapolis, about 1 h 25 min after leaving JFK, or 3 h 35 min after leaving SAN. There, 1,005 of 4,676 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is JFK to SAN a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the JFK–SAN route filed 32,307 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 135 of 32,307 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between JFK and SAN?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, about 3 h 20 min after leaving JFK, or 1 h 40 min after leaving SAN. The next most were over Indiana, near Indianapolis, about 1 h 25 min after leaving JFK, or 3 h 35 min after leaving SAN.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly JFK to SAN?

Moderate bumps made up a similar share of reports all year. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is SAN to JFK the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from SAN to JFK usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at JFK and SAN?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. JFK had gusts of 25 knots or more on 157 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days). SAN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 20 days in the past year, most often in February (4 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
