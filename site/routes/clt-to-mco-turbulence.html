Charlotte ⇄ Orlando

CLT to MCO turbulence

Flights between Charlotte (CLT) and Orlando (MCO) fly about 470 miles, roughly 1¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Georgia, and more often in December, February and March.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 21 of 6,503 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.3%). Light chop was the level reported most: 3,014 reports.

Questions

Is CLT to MCO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the CLT–MCO route filed 6,503 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 21 of 6,503 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between CLT and MCO?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Georgia, near Savannah, about 35 min after leaving CLT, or 35 min after leaving MCO. The next most were over South Carolina, near Savannah, about 25 min after leaving CLT, or 45 min after leaving MCO.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly CLT to MCO?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in December, February and March. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is MCO to CLT the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at CLT and MCO?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. CLT had gusts of 25 knots or more on 49 days in the past year, most often in February (8 days). MCO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 61 days in the past year, most often in October (9 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 6,503 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct CLT–MCO path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
