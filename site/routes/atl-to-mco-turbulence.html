ATL to MCO turbulence

Flights between Atlanta (ATL) and Orlando (MCO) fly about 400 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Georgia, and more often in December, February and April. Severe reports were rare: 21 of 7,070.

Questions

Is ATL to MCO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ATL–MCO route filed 7,070 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 21 of 7,070 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. When is the smoothest time of year to fly ATL to MCO?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in December, February and April. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is MCO to ATL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ATL and MCO?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ATL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 65 days in the past year, most often in March (10 days). MCO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 61 days in the past year, most often in October (9 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 7,070 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct ATL–MCO path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
