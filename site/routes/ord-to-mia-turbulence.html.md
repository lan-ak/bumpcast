# ORD to MIA turbulence

> Chicago (ORD) ⇄ Miami (MIA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Chicago (ORD) and Miami (MIA) fly about 1,200 miles, roughly 2¾ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Indiana, and more often in February, March and April.

- Distance: 1,200 miles (1,930 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 40 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 38,072
- Severe reports: 109 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: ORD 123, MIA 58

## Questions

### Is ORD to MIA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ORD–MIA route filed 38,072 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 109 of 38,072 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between ORD and MIA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Indiana, near Indianapolis, about 25 min after leaving ORD, or 2 h 10 min after leaving MIA. The next most were over Kentucky, near Louisville, about 45 min after leaving ORD, or 1 h 50 min after leaving MIA.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ORD to MIA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in February, March and April. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is MIA to ORD the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ORD and MIA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). MIA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 58 days in the past year, most often in April (9 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
