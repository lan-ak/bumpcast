# DEN to ASE turbulence

> Denver (DEN) ⇄ Aspen (ASE), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Denver (DEN) and Aspen (ASE) fly about 130 miles, roughly 30 minutes in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, and more often in December and June.

- Distance: 130 miles (200 km)
- In the air: about 30 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 8,969
- Severe reports: 90 (1.0%)
- Gusty days: DEN 155, ASE 105

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Colorado, near Denver, about 5 min after leaving DEN, or 25 min after leaving ASE. There, 319 of 540 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over the mountains stirs the air, and gusty days at DEN add to it.

## Takeoff and landing

DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). ASE had gusts of 25 knots or more on 105 days in the past year, most often in June (20 days).

## Questions

### Is DEN to ASE a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DEN–ASE route filed 8,969 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 90 of 8,969 (1.0%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between DEN and ASE?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, near Denver, about 5 min after leaving DEN, or 25 min after leaving ASE.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly DEN to ASE?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in August, September and October and a bigger share in December and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is ASE to DEN the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DEN and ASE?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). ASE had gusts of 25 knots or more on 105 days in the past year, most often in June (20 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
