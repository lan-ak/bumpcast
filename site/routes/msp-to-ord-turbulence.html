MSP to ORD turbulence

Flights between Minneapolis (MSP) and Chicago (ORD) fly about 330 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Wisconsin, and more often in April, May and June. Severe reports were rare: 23 of 5,323.

Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Wisconsin, about 30 min after leaving MSP (25 min after leaving ORD): 172 of 837 reports there. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms. More on clear-air turbulence and turbulence near clouds.

The next most were near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 55 min after leaving MSP (5 min after leaving ORD): 104 of 904 reports there. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at ORD add to it. More on bumps near the ground and gusty takeoffs and landings.

Takeoff and landing

MSP had gusts of 25 knots or more on 127 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). Questions

Is MSP to ORD a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the MSP–ORD route filed 5,323 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 23 of 5,323 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between MSP and ORD?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Wisconsin (about 30 min after leaving MSP (25 min after leaving ORD)), and next most near ORD, where flights climb out or come in to land (about 55 min after leaving MSP (5 min after leaving ORD)).

When is the smoothest time of year to fly MSP to ORD?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in November, December and January and a bigger share in April, May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is ORD to MSP the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at MSP and ORD?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. MSP had gusts of 25 knots or more on 127 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days). ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 5,323 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct MSP–ORD path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
