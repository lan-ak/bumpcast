# LGA to MIA turbulence

> New York (LGA) ⇄ Miami (MIA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between New York (LGA) and Miami (MIA) fly about 1,100 miles, roughly 2½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Virginia, and more often in November, December and February.

- Distance: 1,100 miles (1,770 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 25 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 11,853
- Severe reports: 90 (0.8%)
- Gusty days: LGA 146, MIA 58

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Virginia, near Norfolk, about 40 min after leaving LGA, or 1 h 40 min after leaving MIA. There, 492 of 739 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

The next most were near LGA, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving LGA, or 2 h 20 min after leaving MIA. There, 383 of 825 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at LGA add to it.

## Questions

### Is LGA to MIA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LGA–MIA route filed 11,853 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 90 of 11,853 (0.8%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between LGA and MIA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Virginia, near Norfolk, about 40 min after leaving LGA, or 1 h 40 min after leaving MIA. The next most were near LGA, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving LGA, or 2 h 20 min after leaving MIA.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly LGA to MIA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in November, December and February. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is MIA to LGA the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LGA and MIA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LGA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 146 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days). MIA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 58 days in the past year, most often in April (9 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
