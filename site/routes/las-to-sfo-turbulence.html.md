# LAS to SFO turbulence

> Las Vegas (LAS) ⇄ San Francisco (SFO), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Las Vegas (LAS) and San Francisco (SFO) fly about 410 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over California, and more often in December. Severe reports were rare: 4 of 952.

- Distance: 410 miles (660 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 5 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 952
- Severe reports: 4 (0.4%)
- Gusty days: LAS 109, SFO 144

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over California, near San Jose, about 50 min after leaving LAS (15 min after leaving SFO): 79 of 130 reports there. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at SFO add to it.

## Questions

### Is LAS to SFO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAS–SFO route filed 952 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 4 of 952 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. ### When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAS to SFO?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in May, July and August and a bigger share in December. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is SFO to LAS the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAS and SFO?

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
