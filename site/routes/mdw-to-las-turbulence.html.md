# MDW to LAS turbulence

> Chicago (MDW) ⇄ Las Vegas (LAS), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Chicago (MDW) and Las Vegas (LAS) fly about 1,520 miles, roughly 3¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, and more often in May, June and October.

- Distance: 1,520 miles (2,440 km)
- In the air: about 3 h 15 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 27,424
- Severe reports: 94 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: MDW 130, LAS 109

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often over Colorado, near Colorado Springs, about 1 h 55 min after leaving MDW, or 1 h 10 min after leaving LAS. There, 674 of 2,028 reports were moderate or stronger. The ground below is high, and wind over mountains makes waves that reach cruising height.

The next most were near MDW, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 10 min after leaving MDW, or 3 h 5 min after leaving LAS. There, 579 of 3,179 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over buildings and land stirs the air, and gusty days at MDW add to it.

## Questions

### Is MDW to LAS a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the MDW–LAS route filed 27,424 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 94 of 27,424 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between MDW and LAS?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Colorado, near Colorado Springs, about 1 h 55 min after leaving MDW, or 1 h 10 min after leaving LAS. The next most were near MDW, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 10 min after leaving MDW, or 3 h 5 min after leaving LAS.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly MDW to LAS?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July and August and a bigger share in May, June and October. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is LAS to MDW the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from LAS to MDW usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at MDW and LAS?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. MDW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 130 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days). LAS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 109 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
