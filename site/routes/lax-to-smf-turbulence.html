Los Angeles ⇄ Sacramento

LAX to SMF turbulence

Flights between Los Angeles (LAX) and Sacramento (SMF) fly about 370 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over California, and more often in December and March.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 10 of 939 pilot reports near this route were severe (1.1%). Moderate was the level reported most: 505 reports.

Takeoff and landing

LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days). SMF had gusts of 25 knots or more on 33 days in the past year, most often in May (7 days). Questions

Is LAX to SMF a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAX–SMF route filed 939 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 10 of 939 (1.1%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between LAX and SMF?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over California, near Burbank, about 5 min after leaving LAX, or 55 min after leaving SMF.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAX to SMF?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in May, August and September and a bigger share in December and March. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is SMF to LAX the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAX and SMF?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days). SMF had gusts of 25 knots or more on 33 days in the past year, most often in May (7 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 939 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct LAX–SMF path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
