Los Angeles ⇄ Oakland

LAX to OAK turbulence

Flights between Los Angeles (LAX) and Oakland (OAK) fly about 340 miles, roughly an hour in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near LAX, and more often in December and March.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 13 of 841 pilot reports near this route were severe (1.5%). Moderate was the level reported most: 457 reports.

Takeoff and landing

LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days). OAK had gusts of 25 knots or more on 24 days in the past year, most often in January (4 days). Questions

Is LAX to OAK a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LAX–OAK route filed 841 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 13 of 841 (1.5%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between LAX and OAK?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near LAX, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 5 min after leaving LAX, or 55 min after leaving OAK.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly LAX to OAK?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in February, April and August and a bigger share in December and March. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is OAK to LAX the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LAX and OAK?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LAX had gusts of 25 knots or more on 17 days in the past year, most often in February (5 days). OAK had gusts of 25 knots or more on 24 days in the past year, most often in January (4 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 841 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct LAX–OAK path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
