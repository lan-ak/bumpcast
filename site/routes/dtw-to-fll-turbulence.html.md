# DTW to FLL turbulence

> Detroit (DTW) ⇄ Fort Lauderdale (FLL), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Detroit (DTW) and Fort Lauderdale (FLL) fly about 1,130 miles, roughly 2½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Kentucky, and more often in December, February and March.

- Distance: 1,130 miles (1,820 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 30 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 26,571
- Severe reports: 73 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: DTW 106, FLL 68

## Questions

### Is DTW to FLL a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DTW–FLL route filed 26,571 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 73 of 26,571 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between DTW and FLL?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Kentucky, near Columbus, about 35 min after leaving DTW, or 1 h 50 min after leaving FLL. The next most were over North Carolina, near Charlotte, about 1 h after leaving DTW, or 1 h 20 min after leaving FLL.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly DTW to FLL?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, August and September and a bigger share in December, February and March. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is FLL to DTW the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DTW and FLL?

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
