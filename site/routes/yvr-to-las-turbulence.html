Vancouver ⇄ Las Vegas

YVR to LAS turbulence

Flights between Vancouver (YVR) and Las Vegas (LAS) fly about 990 miles, roughly 2¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Nevada, and more often in December.

Strong bumps are rare

Only 4 of 2,445 pilot reports near this route were severe (0.2%). Moderate was the level reported most: 1,396 reports.

Questions

Is YVR to LAS a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the YVR–LAS route filed 2,445 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 4 of 2,445 (0.2%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

Where is it bumpiest between YVR and LAS?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Nevada, about 1 h 25 min after leaving YVR, or 45 min after leaving LAS. The next most were over or near Nevada, about 1 h 45 min after leaving YVR, or 25 min after leaving LAS.

When is the smoothest time of year to fly YVR to LAS?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in August and October and a bigger share in December. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

Is LAS to YVR the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from YVR to LAS usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at YVR and LAS?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. YVR had gusts of 25 knots or more on 32 days in the past year, most often in December (8 days). LAS had gusts of 25 knots or more on 109 days in the past year, most often in April (17 days).

How we made this page

  • Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, through the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive at Iowa State University.
  • 2,445 pilot reports of turbulence from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct YVR–LAS path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model: a typical climb, cruise and descent). Real flights wander off the direct path, so this is the air near the route, not a record of every flight on it.
  • Airliners only: mainline and regional jets and regional turboprops, not light aircraft. Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "light to moderate" is kept separate, and "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
  • Gusty days: days with at least one hourly airport report of gusts of 25 knots or more, the same threshold the app uses for a gusty takeoff or landing.
  • What the numbers can't say: pilots don't report on every flight, and habits differ: in the past year, smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports filed with Washington Center but 45% with Miami Center. So these pages show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for your flight, and we don't rank routes against each other.
