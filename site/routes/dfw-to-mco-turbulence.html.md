# DFW to MCO turbulence

> Dallas-Fort Worth (DFW) ⇄ Orlando (MCO), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Dallas-Fort Worth (DFW) and Orlando (MCO) fly about 980 miles, roughly 2¼ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Louisiana, and more often in November, December and April.

- Distance: 980 miles (1,580 km)
- In the air: about 2 h 15 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 12,831
- Severe reports: 48 (0.4%)
- Gusty days: DFW 122, MCO 61

## Questions

### Is DFW to MCO a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DFW–MCO route filed 12,831 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 48 of 12,831 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between DFW and MCO?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Louisiana, about 35 min after leaving DFW, or 1 h 30 min after leaving MCO.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly DFW to MCO?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in July, September and October and a bigger share in November, December and April. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is MCO to DFW the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from DFW to MCO usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DFW and MCO?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DFW had gusts of 25 knots or more on 122 days in the past year, most often in March (21 days). MCO had gusts of 25 knots or more on 61 days in the past year, most often in October (9 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
