# DEN to ORD turbulence

> Denver (DEN) ⇄ Chicago (ORD), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Denver (DEN) and Chicago (ORD) fly about 890 miles, roughly 2 hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, and more often in May and June. Severe reports were rare: 74 of 20,138.

- Distance: 890 miles (1,430 km)
- In the air: about 2 h (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 20,138
- Severe reports: 74 (0.4%)
- Gusty days: DEN 155, ORD 123

## Questions

### Is DEN to ORD a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the DEN–ORD route filed 20,138 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 74 of 20,138 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between DEN and ORD?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land (about 5 min after leaving DEN (1 h 55 min after leaving ORD)).

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly DEN to ORD?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in August and September and a bigger share in May and June. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is ORD to DEN the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from DEN to ORD usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at DEN and ORD?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days). ORD had gusts of 25 knots or more on 123 days in the past year, most often in April (20 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
