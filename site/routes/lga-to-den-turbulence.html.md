# LGA to DEN turbulence

> New York (LGA) ⇄ Denver (DEN), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between New York (LGA) and Denver (DEN) fly about 1,620 miles, roughly 3½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often near DEN.

- Distance: 1,620 miles (2,600 km)
- In the air: about 3 h 25 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 31,332
- Severe reports: 120 (0.4%)
- Gusty days: LGA 146, DEN 155

## Where on the route it gets bumpy

Moderate bumps were reported most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 3 h 15 min after leaving LGA, or 10 min after leaving DEN. There, 962 of 4,280 reports were moderate or stronger. Close to the ground, wind over the mountains stirs the air, and gusty days at DEN add to it.

The next most were over Ohio, near Cleveland, about 50 min after leaving LGA, or 2 h 35 min after leaving DEN. There, 707 of 994 reports were moderate or stronger. At cruising height, bumps usually come from clear-air turbulence near the jet stream, or from clouds and storms.

## Questions

### Is LGA to DEN a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the LGA–DEN route filed 31,332 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 120 of 31,332 (0.4%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between LGA and DEN?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often near DEN, where flights climb out or come in to land, about 3 h 15 min after leaving LGA, or 10 min after leaving DEN. The next most were over Ohio, near Cleveland, about 50 min after leaving LGA, or 2 h 35 min after leaving DEN.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly LGA to DEN?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in September. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is DEN to LGA the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight. Flights from DEN to LGA usually take less time than the way back, with the prevailing westerly winds behind them.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at LGA and DEN?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. LGA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 146 days in the past year, most often in December (20 days). DEN had gusts of 25 knots or more on 155 days in the past year, most often in June (22 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
