# ATL to MIA turbulence

> Atlanta (ATL) ⇄ Miami (MIA), both directions. November 2025 to October 2026.

Flights between Atlanta (ATL) and Miami (MIA) fly about 600 miles, roughly 1½ hours in the air. In the past year, pilots near this route reported moderate bumps most often over Florida, and more often in November, February and April.

- Distance: 600 miles (960 km)
- In the air: about 1 h 30 min (typical jet, still air)
- Pilot reports near the route: 14,041
- Severe reports: 45 (0.3%)
- Gusty days: ATL 65, MIA 58

## Questions

### Is ATL to MIA a bumpy flight?

Most flights see light chop at most. From November 2025 to October 2026, pilots near the ATL–MIA route filed 14,041 turbulence reports, and severe reports were rare: 45 of 14,041 (0.3%). Pilots report bumpy air more readily than smooth, so the share of flights with strong bumps is lower still. On the day, the weather decides: BumpCast forecasts your exact flight from 48 hours before departure.

### Where is it bumpiest between ATL and MIA?

Pilots reported moderate bumps most often over Florida, near Jacksonville, about 40 min after leaving ATL, or 45 min after leaving MIA. The next most were over Georgia, about 25 min after leaving ATL, or 1 h after leaving MIA.

### When is the smoothest time of year to fly ATL to MIA?

In the past year, moderate bumps made up a smaller share of reports in June, July and August and a bigger share in November, February and April. Any month can have a smooth or a bumpy day, so the forecast for your flight matters more.

### Is MIA to ATL the same?

Yes. It's the same air, so bumps are reported in the same places, just at different times into the flight.

### Are takeoffs and landings bumpy at ATL and MIA?

On windy days they can be, for a few minutes. ATL had gusts of 25 knots or more on 65 days in the past year, most often in March (10 days). MIA had gusts of 25 knots or more on 58 days in the past year, most often in April (9 days).

## How this page was made

- Period: November 2025 to October 2026. Pilot reports (PIREPs) and airport weather reports (METARs) from the US National Weather Service, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/gis/pireps.php
- Reports from airliners within 60 nautical miles of the direct path, near the height a flight would be there (BumpCast's flight model). Each report counts at the strongest turbulence it names; "moderate to severe" counts as severe.
- Gusty days: days with at least one hourly report of gusts of 25 knots or more.
- Reporting habits differ: smooth air made up 4% of cruise reports at Washington Center but 45% at Miami Center. These numbers show where and when bumps were reported, compared within a route. They aren't the odds for a given flight, and routes aren't ranked against each other.
