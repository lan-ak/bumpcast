# Clear-air turbulence

> Bumps at cruise, often in clear skies.

Clear-air turbulence is bumpy air at cruising height, often in clear skies, where the fast jet stream flows next to slower air. It's usually light chop.

## What causes clear-air turbulence?

High up, fast rivers of wind (the jet stream) flow next to slower air. Where they meet, the air tumbles a little, like water where a fast stream meets a still pool.

## What do pilots do about clear-air turbulence?

Pilots get forecasts and reports from planes ahead, and often climb or descend a few thousand feet to find smoother air. They turn on the seatbelt sign before it starts.

## What does clear-air turbulence feel like?

Usually light chop: a gentle, rhythmic rocking. Your drink ripples.

## Good to know

This is the most common kind of bump at cruise, and the plane is built for far more than it can produce.

## In more detail

### How fast and how high is the jet stream?

Jet streams are narrow bands of strong wind from about 20,000 to 45,000 feet, the heights where airliners cruise. They blow from west to east and can top 275 miles per hour. The speed itself isn't what makes bumps: it's the sharp change in wind speed at the jet's edges, where the air rolls over in waves.

Source: [Aviation Weather Handbook (FAA-H-8083-28B), 2026](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/FAA-H-8083-28B.pdf)

### When is clear-air turbulence most common?

In winter. Jet streams follow the boundary between warm and cold air, which is sharpest in winter, so that's when they're strongest. Over the US, clear-air turbulence is found most often on the north side of the jet stream, near the top of the lower atmosphere (the tropopause).

Source: [Aviation Weather Handbook (FAA-H-8083-28B), 2026](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/FAA-H-8083-28B.pdf)

### How big is a patch of clear-air turbulence?

Patches near the jet stream are typically 100 to 300 miles long, 50 to 100 miles wide and about 5,000 feet deep, and they can last from half an hour to a day. That's why a climb or descent of a few thousand feet often finds smoother air, and why reports from planes ahead help so much.

Source: [Aviation Weather Handbook (FAA-H-8083-28B), 2026](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/FAA-H-8083-28B.pdf)

### Is it bumpier at cruise in winter?

In pilot reports, yes. In BumpCast's analysis of 320,865 airliner reports at cruising height over the US and southern Canada (November 2025 to October 2026), moderate or stronger bumps made up about 1.3 times as large a share of reports from December to February as from June to August.

Source: [Turbulence by route: a year of pilot reports and airport weather](https://bumpcast.app/routes/)
