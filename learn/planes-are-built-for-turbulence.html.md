# Planes are built for it

> Why turbulence is a comfort issue.

Airliners are designed and tested to handle forces well beyond anything turbulence produces in normal flight. Turbulence is a comfort issue, not a structural one.

## Are planes built to handle turbulence?

Airliners are designed and tested to handle forces well beyond anything turbulence produces in normal flight. Wings flex on purpose; that's why you may see them move.

## What do pilots do when it gets bumpy?

In bumps, pilots slow to a speed that gives the aircraft extra margin and keep flying normally.

## Why do the drops feel so big?

Movement can feel big, but most drops are a change in height of only a few meters.

## In more detail

### How strong are airliners built to be?

US rules for airliners (14 CFR Part 25) set the loads a plane must carry. It must handle at least 2.5 g, two and a half times its weight pressing on the wings, without lasting damage. Then a safety factor of 1.5 goes on top: the structure must hold 1.5 times those loads without breaking. That's 2.5 × 1.5, or 3.75 g.

Source: [14 CFR 25.337: Limit maneuvering load factors](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-25.337); [14 CFR 25.303: Factor of safety](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-25.303); [14 CFR 25.305: Strength and deformation](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-25.305)

### Are planes designed for gusts and turbulence too?

Yes. The same rules require designers to work out the loads from sudden up, down and sideways gusts, and from continuous turbulence, and to build for them. At sea level the design gust is a sudden 56 feet per second (about 38 mph) of moving air.

Source: [14 CFR 25.341: Gust and turbulence loads](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-25.341)
