Why it gets bumpy

Gusty at the airport

On a windy day, the wind near the ground speeds up and slows down over buildings and land. You feel it for a few minutes as you climb out and on the way down.

Why are takeoffs and landings bumpy on windy days?

On a windy day, the wind near the ground speeds up and slows down as it flows over buildings and land, a bit like a river over stones. You feel it for a few minutes as you climb out and on the way down.

What do pilots do in gusty wind?

Pilots plan every takeoff and landing around the wind. In gusty air they fly the approach a little faster for extra control, and keep the seatbelt sign on until the air settles.

What does a gusty takeoff or landing feel like?

Bumps and some side-to-side sway, now and then a firmer one, that ease as you climb or once you touch down.

Good to know

Gusty takeoffs and landings happen every day at busy airports. Some airports don't have a wind forecast, or not until a few hours before. Your forecast still covers the climb and descent either way.

In more detail

How are gusts reported at airports?

In an airport's weather report (a METAR), a G after the wind speed gives the gusts: 22015G25KT means wind from 220 degrees at 15 knots, gusting to 25 knots. BumpCast calls an end of your flight gusty when the airport's forecast has gusts of 25 knots or more.

Source: Aeronautical Information Manual, chapter 7, section 1: Meteorology

Why do pilots fly a little faster in gusty wind?

Extra speed gives extra control when the wind keeps changing. A common technique taught to pilots is to add half the gust factor to the normal approach speed: with gusts 15 knots above the steady wind, about 7 knots more. Airline crews follow their aircraft maker's and airline's own procedures, which also add a little speed in gusty air.

Source: Airplane Flying Handbook (FAA-H-8083-3C), chapter 9: Approaches and Landings

Which airports have the most gusty days?

Of the 22 airports on BumpCast's route pages, the ones with the most days with gusts of 25 knots or more in November 2025 to October 2026 were JFK (157 days), DEN (155 days) and BOS (148 days). The fewest were LAX (17 days), YVR (32 days) and SEA (48 days).

Source: Turbulence by route: a year of pilot reports and airport weather
